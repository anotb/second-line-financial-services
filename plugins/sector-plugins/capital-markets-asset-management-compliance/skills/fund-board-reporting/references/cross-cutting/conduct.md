# Conduct cross-cutting overlay for fund-board-reporting

When the engagement-scope record flags `conduct` for the fund-board pack, this overlay sets the conduct-content for the named pack sections. Conduct in the fund-board context is the fiduciary lens — the adviser's fiduciary duty under §206 of the Investment Advisers Act, the affiliated-transaction conflict surface under §17 of the Investment Company Act, the §15(c) factor analysis itself (which is a fiduciary-bargain test), and the soft-dollar, brokerage, and revenue-sharing surfaces that drive fall-out benefits to the adviser and affiliates.

Loaded by: `fund-board-reporting/SKILL.md` when the engagement scope flags conduct, when the pack covers a §15(c) renewal cycle (which always invokes the fiduciary lens), or when affiliated-transaction or soft-dollar items in the period rise to a level that warrants substantive board treatment beyond the standing affiliated-transactions table.

## Source basis

- IA Act §206 — antifraud and fiduciary duty of the investment adviser; SEC Commission Interpretation Regarding Standard of Conduct for Investment Advisers (Release No. IA-5248, June 5, 2019).
- ICA §17 — affiliated transactions:
  - §17(a) — prohibitions on affiliated transactions.
  - §17(d) and Rule 17d-1 — joint transactions with affiliated persons.
  - §17(e) and Rule 17e-1 — affiliated brokerage; reasonable-and-fair commission determination; quarterly board procedure-adequacy review.
  - Rule 17a-7 — affiliated cross trades; pricing methodology; board procedure adoption and annual review.
  - Rule 17a-8 — affiliated mergers.
- ICA §15(c) — advisory contract approval and renewal under the Gartenberg / Jones v. Harris factor analysis; the §15(c) deliberation is itself the principal documented fiduciary-bargain record.
- Securities Exchange Act §28(e) — soft-dollar safe harbor; SEC Release No. 34-54165 (July 18, 2006) interpretive guidance on client commission practices.
- IA Act Rule 204A-1 — adviser code of ethics; reaches sub-advisers under fund-complex sub-adviser oversight loop.
- ICA Rule 17j-1 — fund code of ethics; access-person personal trading reporting; board approval.
- 12b-1 / Rule 12b-1 — fund distribution payments; board approval and ongoing oversight; inherently a fall-out-benefit consideration in §15(c).

## What changes for conduct-flagged fund-board packs

### 1. The §15(c) factor analysis as fiduciary-bargain record

The §15(c) renewal pack is itself the principal conduct artifact. The Gartenberg / Jones v. Harris factor analysis tests whether the advisory bargain is one that could have resulted from arm's-length negotiation. The conduct overlay reinforces:

- Profitability of the adviser from the fund relationship: methodology pin (direct expense allocation, indirect cost basis, capital usage). A bare profitability number without methodology is the §36(b) discovery finding pattern.
- Fall-out benefits to the adviser and affiliates: the catalogue must be substantive — soft-dollar credits and the eligibility analysis under §28(e); affiliated-broker revenue under Rule 17e-1; 12b-1 distribution flows to the adviser's distributor affiliate; affiliated-bank deposit balances and cash-management revenue; sub-adviser-relationship economics where the sub-adviser is affiliated; revenue-sharing flows from the adviser to financial intermediaries (where the adviser pays for distribution and shelf space).
- Comparative fees and expenses: peer-group selection methodology; address Jones v. Harris "comparable services" question for the adviser's institutional-account or sub-adviser-relationship fees against the fund-level fee for comparable services.
- Economies of scale and breakpoints: AUM growth analysis; written analysis of why no further breakpoint is appropriate where AUM has grown materially since the last breakpoint adjustment.
- Trustee questions and adviser responses: substantive challenge documented; recycled prior-year questions without period-specific updates is itself a finding pattern.

### 2. Affiliated-transaction conflict surface

Affiliated transactions under §17 carry an inherent conflict that the board manages through procedure-adequacy determinations. The conduct overlay reinforces:

- Rule 17e-1 affiliated brokerage: aggregate commissions to affiliated brokers in the period; the board's quarterly procedure-adequacy determination (§17e-1(a)(3)) is on the cycle every cycle; commentary on any procedure exception or change.
- Rule 17a-7 affiliated cross trades: count and aggregate value; pricing methodology evidence for each cross trade against the §17a-7 conditions (current-market price determination; compliance with the four conditions); annual board review of the procedures (§17a-7(e)).
- §17(a) and Rule 17a-8 affiliated transactions more broadly: any affiliated merger or significant affiliated transaction in the period, with the board's §17 analysis.
- §17(d) and Rule 17d-1 joint transactions: any in-period joint transaction with an affiliated person and the SEC exemptive-order or Rule 17d-1 reliance basis.
- Related-person disclosures: any new related-person relationship in the period; any change in the §2(a)(19) interested-person status of a board member or officer.

### 3. Soft-dollar and §28(e) disclosure

Soft-dollar use is a fiduciary-relevant board disclosure even where the adviser does not make affiliated-broker payments. The conduct overlay carries:

- Total commissions paid for research and brokerage during the period; allocation between research and execution.
- Adviser's eligibility analysis under §28(e) for the research and brokerage services received; the 2006 SEC interpretive release framework.
- MiFID II hard-dollar treatment for advisers with EU client coverage; any unbundling of the fund's commissions.
- Commission-sharing arrangements (CSA) and client-commission arrangements (CCA) in use.
- Any policy change in the period, including any change in eligible-research categories or eligible-brokerage categories.
- Soft-dollar credits as a fall-out benefit in the §15(c) analysis (cross-link to the §15(c) section).

### 4. Code of ethics and personal-trading conduct

Rule 17j-1 (fund code of ethics) and Rule 204A-1 (adviser code of ethics) reach access persons of the fund and the adviser, including sub-advisers under the fund-complex sub-adviser oversight loop. The conduct overlay carries:

- Aggregate personal-trading violations and code of ethics violations in the period (count and severity; individual employee names do not appear in the pack).
- Material code of ethics violations that meet the §38a-1 material-compliance-matter threshold.
- Sub-adviser code of ethics violations reaching the fund-CCO oversight loop.
- Any code of ethics policy change in the period; CCO recommendation on adequacy.

### 5. 12b-1 distribution arrangements

For funds with a Rule 12b-1 distribution plan, the conduct overlay carries:

- 12b-1 fee aggregate paid in the period and the use of the fee (distribution payments, shareholder services, broker-dealer compensation, intermediary payments).
- The board's quarterly review of payments under the 12b-1 plan and the annual continuance determination.
- The fall-out-benefit treatment of 12b-1 flows to the adviser's distributor affiliate (cross-link to §15(c) fall-out benefits).
- Any 12b-1 plan amendment in the period, including the board approval and the shareholder-vote requirement analysis.

### 6. Marketing Rule conduct surface

Rule 206(4)-1 (Marketing Rule) is the adviser's conduct surface for performance presentation, hypothetical performance, testimonials and endorsements, third-party ratings, and case studies. Material findings affecting fund marketing land in the §38a-1 service-provider compliance program assessment for the adviser and in the §15(c) nature-extent-quality factor.

Detail of marketing-rule evidence and substantiation lives in `marketing-rule-evidence`.

### 7. Fiduciary-relevant forward-looking content

The forward-looking commentary's conduct component covers items the trustees should be aware of:

- Pending or in-flight SEC adoption activity that bears on adviser conduct or fund-affiliate transactions.
- Recent enforcement themes affecting fund advisers (off-channel-communications enforcement, marketing-rule enforcement on hypothetical performance and testimonials, fee-litigation themes under §36(b)).
- SEC Division of Examinations focus areas for the coming cycle that bear on fiduciary or affiliated-transaction review.
- Any in-flight SEC examination or enforcement engagement with the adviser or the fund.

## Conduct-specific output content

Added to the fund-board pack content:

- §15(c) advisory contract renewal pack content (annual cycle): full Gartenberg / Jones v. Harris factor analysis as the central fiduciary-bargain record; methodology pin for profitability and peer-group; substantive trustee questions and adviser responses.
- Affiliated transactions section: Rule 17e-1 affiliated brokerage with quarterly procedure-adequacy determination; Rule 17a-7 cross trades with pricing methodology; §17(a) / Rule 17a-8 / §17(d) / Rule 17d-1 in-period activity; related-person disclosures.
- Soft-dollar / §28(e) disclosure: commission allocation; eligibility analysis; CSA/CCA; MiFID II treatment; policy change.
- Code of ethics summary: aggregate violations; material code matters; sub-adviser code matters; policy change.
- 12b-1 plan oversight content where applicable: aggregate fee; use of fee; quarterly review; annual continuance; fall-out treatment.
- Forward-looking conduct items.

## Common pitfalls in conduct-flagged fund-board packs

- §15(c) profitability number without a defined methodology. The methodology is the fiduciary-bargain record; the number alone fails the Gartenberg / Jones v. Harris substantive review.
- Fall-out benefits catalogued generically. The adviser's fall-out benefits include soft-dollar credits, affiliated-broker revenue, 12b-1 distribution flows, affiliated-bank deposit balances, cash-management revenue, sub-adviser-relationship economics, and revenue-sharing payments. A generic statement misses the substantive question.
- Trustee questions recycled from the prior year. Independent trustees' questions are the documented substantive challenge; period-specific updates demonstrate the §15(c) deliberation; recycled questions are themselves a finding pattern in §36(b) discovery.
- Rule 17a-7 cross trades reported as a count. Each trade should carry the pricing methodology evidence against the §17a-7 four conditions; the board's procedure adoption and annual review is required by §17a-7(e).
- Rule 17e-1 affiliated brokerage reported as an aggregate without the board's quarterly procedure-adequacy determination on file. The quarterly determination is a §17e-1 condition; the pack reflects whether the determination was made.
- Soft-dollar disclosure treated as a generic statement of policy. The commission allocation, eligibility analysis, and any policy change in the period is the substantive disclosure.
- Code of ethics violations reported with individual employee names. Aggregate counts and material-matter detail without individual identification is the standard; HR / Legal channels carry individual-employee detail.
- 12b-1 quarterly review and annual continuance determinations missing from the pack for funds with a 12b-1 plan. The plan's continuance is a board action; the pack carries the supporting record.
- Conflating §206 fiduciary duty (adviser-level) with §36(b) excessive-fee analysis (board-level deliberation under §15(c) factors). They run together but are not the same; the §15(c) record carries both lenses.
