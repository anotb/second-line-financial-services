## Primary anchors

### 1. Investment Company Act §15(c) — Advisory contract approval and the §15(c) factors

- Citation: 15 USC §80a-15(c) (Investment Company Act of 1940 §15(c)). Implementing regulatory and judicial gloss: Gartenberg v. Merrill Lynch Asset Management, Inc., 694 F.2d 923 (2d Cir. 1982); Jones v. Harris Associates L.P., 559 U.S. 335 (2010).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: §15(c) requires that the board of a registered investment company, including a majority of trustees who are not interested persons, request and evaluate such information as may reasonably be necessary to evaluate the terms of an advisory contract before approving or renewing it. Gartenberg, applied and clarified by Jones v. Harris, sets the factors a court will use to assess whether the fee bargain was reached at arm's length: nature, extent, and quality of services; investment performance; profitability of the adviser from the fund relationship; economies of scale and breakpoints; fall-out benefits to the adviser and affiliates; and comparative fees and expenses against a peer set. Boards typically request these inputs annually as the §15(c) materials request and document the deliberation in board minutes.
- Link: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2022-title15/html/USCODE-2022-title15-chap2D-subchapI-sec80a-15.htm — 15 USC §80a-15(c)
- Notes: Excessive-fee litigation under §36(b) of the Act consumes the same factor record. Documenting the deliberation against the named factors with methodology pinned (peer-group selection, profitability allocation method, fall-out benefit catalogue) is the practitioner standard.

### 2. Rule 38a-1 — Compliance programs of investment companies

- Citation: 17 CFR §270.38a-1 (Investment Company Act Rule 38a-1), adopted in SEC Release No. IC-26299 (December 17, 2003). Companion adviser rule: 17 CFR §275.206(4)-7 (Investment Advisers Act Rule 206(4)-7).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Rule 38a-1 requires every registered investment company to adopt and implement written policies and procedures reasonably designed to prevent violation of the federal securities laws by the fund, including policies and procedures providing for the oversight of compliance by each investment adviser, principal underwriter, administrator, and transfer agent of the fund. Subsection (a)(2) requires board approval of policies and procedures, including those of each service provider. Subsection (a)(3) requires an annual review of the adequacy and effectiveness of the policies and procedures. Subsection (a)(4) designates the chief compliance officer, requires the CCO to report directly to the board, requires the CCO to deliver a written annual report to the board on the operation of the fund's compliance program and the compliance programs of each service provider including any material compliance matters since the prior report and any material changes to the policies and procedures, and protects the CCO from removal without board approval.
- Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-270/section-270.38a-1 — 17 CFR §270.38a-1
- Notes: The "material compliance matter" definition in Rule 38a-1(e)(2) covers violations of the federal securities laws, violations of the policies and procedures, and weaknesses in the design or implementation of the policies and procedures. The CCO's annual written report is the core artifact this skill assembles.

### 3. Investment Company Act §17(e) and Rule 17e-1 — Affiliated brokerage

- Citation: 15 USC §80a-17(e) (Investment Company Act §17(e)); 17 CFR §270.17e-1 (Rule 17e-1).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: §17(e) restricts the receipt of compensation by affiliated persons of a registered investment company in connection with the purchase or sale of property to or from the fund and limits the commissions an affiliated broker may receive on fund portfolio transactions. Rule 17e-1 sets the conditions under which an affiliated broker may receive commissions on fund agency transactions, including a board-adopted procedure for reviewing the reasonableness of the commission and the requirement that the board (including a majority of independent trustees) determine no less frequently than quarterly that the procedures are reasonably designed to provide that the commission is reasonable and fair. The board pack typically reports the §17(e) / Rule 17e-1 affiliated brokerage activity for the period and the procedure-adequacy determination.
- Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-270/section-270.17e-1 — 17 CFR §270.17e-1
- Notes: Rule 17a-7 (affiliated cross trades) and §17(a) (affiliated transactions more broadly) sit alongside §17(e) and frequently appear in the same affiliated-transactions section of the board pack.

### 4. Securities Exchange Act §28(e) — Soft-dollar safe harbor

- Citation: 15 USC §78bb(e) (Securities Exchange Act of 1934 §28(e)). SEC interpretive guidance: SEC Release No. 34-54165, "Commission Guidance Regarding Client Commission Practices Under Section 28(e) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934" (July 18, 2006).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: §28(e) provides a safe harbor permitting a money manager to pay more than the lowest available commission for brokerage if the manager determines in good faith that the commission is reasonable in relation to the value of the brokerage and research services received. The 2006 SEC interpretive release narrowed the eligible-research and eligible-brokerage categories and set the framework for client-commission arrangements (CCA) and commission-sharing arrangements (CSA). Fund boards typically receive a §28(e) / soft-dollar disclosure section reporting the adviser's use of fund commissions for research and brokerage, the eligibility analysis, and any policy changes.
- Link: https://www.sec.gov/rules/interp/2006/34-54165.pdf — SEC Release No. 34-54165, Section II (Eligible Research and Brokerage)
- Notes: §28(e) operates alongside the IA fiduciary duty and best-execution obligation. MiFID II unbundling has driven many global advisers to pay for research with hard dollars; the board-pack disclosure should reflect the actual practice, not a generic statement.

### 5. Rule 22e-4 — Investment Company Liquidity Risk Management Programs

- Citation: 17 CFR §270.22e-4. Adopted in SEC Release No. IC-32315 (October 13, 2016); compliance dates phased through 2018-2019. Subsequent SEC reporting and disclosure changes flagged separately.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Rule 22e-4 requires every open-end management investment company (other than money market funds) to adopt and implement a written liquidity risk management program reasonably designed to assess and manage the fund's liquidity risk. Required program elements include classification of each portfolio investment into one of four liquidity buckets (highly liquid, moderately liquid, less liquid, illiquid), determination and periodic review of a highly liquid investment minimum (HLIM) where applicable, a 15% limit on illiquid investments, and board approval and oversight of the program. The board approves the program initially and any material changes; receives the program administrator's annual written report on the adequacy and effectiveness of the program; and receives prompt reports of certain breaches and conditions.
- Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-270/section-270.22e-4 — 17 CFR §270.22e-4
- Notes: SEC has proposed and re-proposed amendments to Rule 22e-4 and related fund liquidity reporting (Release No. IC-34746, November 2, 2022, "Open-End Fund Liquidity Risk Management Programs and Swing Pricing"); current state of the proposal must be verified at pack-write time. ### 6. Rule 2a-5 — Good faith determinations of fair value

- Citation: 17 CFR §270.2a-5. Adopted in SEC Release No. IC-34128 (December 3, 2020); compliance date September 8, 2022. Companion Rule 31a-4 sets recordkeeping requirements.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Rule 2a-5 sets the framework for good-faith determinations of fair value under §2(a)(41) of the Investment Company Act. The board may either perform fair-value determinations itself or designate the determination to a "valuation designee" (typically the adviser or a sub-adviser), subject to board oversight. The rule sets required functions including periodic assessment and management of valuation risks, establishment and application of fair-value methodologies, testing of fair-value methodologies, and oversight and evaluation of pricing services. Where the board designates a valuation designee, the rule requires periodic and prompt board reporting on the designee's performance, including a quarterly written report and prompt reporting (no later than five business days after the valuation designee becomes aware) of matters that materially affect the fair value of the designated portfolio of investments.
- Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-270/section-270.2a-5 — 17 CFR §270.2a-5
- Notes: Rule 2a-5 superseded Accounting Series Releases 113 and 118 to the extent inconsistent. The valuation designee report and the prompt-notification log are now standard board-pack content.

### 7. Rule 18f-4 — Use of derivatives by registered investment companies

- Citation: 17 CFR §270.18f-4. Adopted in SEC Release No. IC-34084 (October 28, 2020); compliance date August 19, 2022.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Rule 18f-4 permits a registered investment company or business development company to enter into derivatives transactions and certain other transactions notwithstanding §18 of the Investment Company Act, subject to a derivatives risk management program and a value-at-risk (VaR) based limit. Required program elements include designation of a derivatives risk manager, identification and assessment of derivatives risks, establishment of risk guidelines, stress testing, backtesting, internal reporting and escalation, and program review. The fund board approves the designation of the derivatives risk manager and receives the derivatives risk manager's written report at least annually on the program and any material risks arising from the fund's derivatives transactions, and receives reports on VaR-limit breaches. A "limited derivatives user" exemption is available for funds with derivatives exposure below 10% of net assets, subject to alternative requirements.
- Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-270/section-270.18f-4 — 17 CFR §270.18f-4
- Notes: Limited-derivatives-user status can transition mid-period if the 10% threshold is exceeded; the board pack should reflect either continuous compliance or a documented transition to the full DRMP.

### 8. Investment Company Act §10(a) — Board independence

- Citation: 15 USC §80a-10(a) (Investment Company Act §10(a)). Related: §2(a)(19) (definition of "interested person"); Rule 0-1(a)(7) (definition of "independent legal counsel").
- Excerpt-paraphrase: §10(a) requires that no more than 60% of the board of directors of a registered investment company be interested persons (as defined in §2(a)(19)). Many fund complexes operate with a substantially higher proportion of independent trustees; the SEC Investment Company Act exemptive-rule package (the "Independent Director" rules including Rule 10f-3, Rule 17a-7, Rule 17a-8, Rule 17d-1, Rule 17e-1, Rule 18f-3, and others) typically conditions reliance on a 75% independent-trustee composition and other board-process requirements.
- Link: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2022-title15/html/USCODE-2022-title15-chap2D-subchapI-sec80a-10.htm — 15 USC §80a-10(a)
- Notes: Board-composition disclosure feeds Form N-CEN and the fund's registration statement; this skill consumes the composition fact, does not assert it.

### 9. Form N-CEN — Annual report for registered investment companies

- Citation: SEC Form N-CEN. Adopted in SEC Release Nos. 33-10231, IC-32314 (October 13, 2016); compliance date June 1, 2018. 17 CFR §270.30a-1 sets the filing requirement.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Form N-CEN is the annual structured-data census filing for registered investment companies, replacing the legacy Form N-SAR. The form captures fund-level identifying information, service-provider identification (adviser, sub-adviser, administrator, transfer agent, custodian, distributor, pricing service, independent public accountant), board composition, audit and oversight committee composition, and summary information on fund operations including securities lending, affiliated transactions, and certain rule-reliance disclosures (including reliance on Rule 18f-4 limited-derivatives-user status and Rule 17a-7 affiliated cross trades). The fund-board pack typically references the N-CEN content the adviser will submit, particularly the rule-reliance disclosures and the service-provider identification, before the filing window.
- Link: https://www.sec.gov/files/formn-cen.pdf — Form N-CEN form text and General Instructions [verify form-version date at pack-write time]
- Notes: N-CEN is filed within 75 days after fiscal year end (60 days for unit investment trusts). The board pack does not file N-CEN; it identifies the data the adviser will submit and surfaces any rule-reliance items the board should be aware of.

### 10. Form N-CSR — Certified shareholder report

- Citation: SEC Form N-CSR. Adopted in SEC Release Nos. 33-8238, 34-47986, IC-26068 (June 5, 2003), implementing the Sarbanes-Oxley Act §302 and §906 certification requirements for registered investment companies. 17 CFR §270.30b2-1 sets the filing requirement.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Form N-CSR is the certified shareholder report filing for registered investment companies. It contains the shareholder report (semi-annual), CEO/CFO certifications under SOX §302 and §906, and disclosures including the fund's principal officer and audit committee disclosures. The report is filed within 10 days after transmittal of the report to shareholders. The SEC's Tailored Shareholder Report rules (Release Nos. 33-11125, IC-34731, October 26, 2022; compliance date July 24, 2024) restructured the shareholder-report content for open-end funds (other than money market funds), requiring concise fund-specific reports and moving certain detail to Form N-CSR appendices and the SEC's website.
- Link: https://www.sec.gov/files/formn-csr.pdf — Form N-CSR form text [verify form-version date at pack-write time]
- Notes: The Tailored Shareholder Report rules apply on a fund-by-fund basis to open-end funds other than money market funds. ETFs, closed-end funds, BDCs, and money market funds remain on the prior shareholder-report regime. The board oversees the disclosure process; the pack tracks the disclosure-program status, particularly during the initial implementation cycle.

### 11. Rule 35d-1 — Names Rule

- Citation: 17 CFR §270.35d-1 (Investment Company Act Rule 35d-1). Original rule adopted 2001 (Release No. IC-24828, January 17, 2001); 2023 amendments adopted in SEC Release No. IC-35000 (September 20, 2023). **Compliance dates extended (SEC March 14, 2025): large fund groups ($1B+ net assets at most recent fiscal-year end) June 11, 2026; smaller fund groups (less than $1B net assets) December 11, 2026. Form N-PORT Names Rule reporting compliance further extended February 2026 to November 17, 2027 (large) / May 18, 2028 (smaller) — N-PORT reporting only, not the underlying Names Rule.**
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Rule 35d-1 governs the use of fund names that suggest a particular type of investment, industry, geographic focus, or tax status, and requires a fund with such a name to adopt a policy to invest at least 80% of its assets in the type of investment, industry, geographic area, or tax-related investment its name suggests. The 2023 amendments expanded the rule's reach to fund names suggesting that the fund focuses on investments that have, or whose issuers have, particular characteristics (including names that incorporate ESG, sustainable, growth, value, and other thematic terminology). The amendments also include compliance-policy and recordkeeping requirements.
- Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-270/section-270.35d-1 — 17 CFR §270.35d-1; https://www.sec.gov/rules/2023/09/investment-company-names — SEC Release No. IC-35000 (September 20, 2023)
- Notes: ESG-labeled fund disclosures, greenwashing risk in shareholder reports, and ESG fund-name compliance feed the climate cross-cutting overlay (when loaded) and the issues-and-management-actions section of the board pack.

### 13. Reg S-P — Privacy of consumer financial information; safeguarding

- Citation: 17 CFR Part 248 (Regulation S-P). May 2024 amendments adopted in SEC Release Nos. 34-100155, IA-6604, IC-35193 (May 16, 2024). Compliance dates: larger entities December 3, 2025; smaller entities June 3, 2026 [verify the larger / smaller-entity scope cut against the operative final-rule definitions].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Reg S-P, as amended in May 2024, requires SEC-registered broker-dealers, investment advisers, registered investment companies, and transfer agents to maintain written incident-response programs reasonably designed to detect, respond to, and recover from unauthorised access to or use of customer information. Covered entities provide notice to affected individuals as soon as practicable, but no later than 30 days after becoming aware that unauthorised access to or use has occurred or is reasonably likely to have occurred. The amendments also expanded the safeguards rule, the disposal rule, and the recordkeeping requirements. Fund-board reporting carries the cyber overlay's enterprise-rollup of Reg S-P incident-response posture and any breach-notice events affecting fund shareholders.
- Link: https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2024/34-100155.pdf — SEC Release Nos. 34-100155, IA-6604, IC-35193 (May 16, 2024)
- Notes: The customer-notice provision covers natural-person customers including fund shareholders. Service-provider breach notification flows up through the adviser to the fund board through the §38a-1 service-provider compliance program oversight loop.

## Judicial gloss

### 14. Gartenberg v. Merrill Lynch Asset Management, Inc.

- Citation: 694 F.2d 923 (2d Cir. 1982).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The Second Circuit set the standard for excessive-fee claims under §36(b) of the Investment Company Act, articulating that to violate §36(b) the adviser must charge a fee that is so disproportionately large that it bears no reasonable relationship to the services rendered and could not have been the product of arm's-length bargaining. The court enumerated the factors that bear on the analysis, which became the "Gartenberg factors" used by §15(c) practitioners: nature, extent, and quality of services; investment performance; profitability; economies of scale; fall-out benefits; and comparative fees and expenses. The case set the practitioner standard for the §15(c) materials request and the board's deliberation record.
- Link: https://casetext.com/case/gartenberg-v-merrill-lynch-asset-management-inc-2 — 694 F.2d 923 (2d Cir. 1982)
- Notes: The §15(c) deliberation is the principal documented record a court reviews in any subsequent §36(b) action; the materials and minutes carry the burden.

### 15. Jones v. Harris Associates L.P.

- Citation: 559 U.S. 335 (2010).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The Supreme Court adopted the Gartenberg standard for §36(b) excessive-fee claims, holding that to be guilty of a violation of §36(b) an investment adviser must charge a fee that is so disproportionately large that it bears no reasonable relationship to the services rendered and could not have been the product of arm's-length bargaining. The Court emphasized that courts should give deference to a board's process where the board's process was the product of careful deliberation conducted by independent and conscientious trustees who were furnished with the information necessary to consider the relevant factors, but that comparisons to fees charged by the same adviser to other types of clients (institutional, sub-adviser arrangements) are relevant where the services rendered are comparable. The case reinforced the practitioner standard for the §15(c) materials and the board record.
- Link: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/559/335/ — 559 U.S. 335 (2010)
- Notes: Comparative-fee analysis in the §15(c) pack should document the peer-group selection methodology and address any differences between the adviser's fund-level fees and the adviser's institutional-account or sub-adviser-relationship fees for comparable services.

## Cross-cutting overlays cited

### 16. NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 (February 2024) and FFIEC IT Examination Handbook

- Citation: NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 (released February 26, 2024); FFIEC Information Technology Examination Handbook, Information Security and Cybersecurity sections. Used for the cyber cross-cutting overlay.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: NIST CSF 2.0 organizes cybersecurity risk management into six functions (Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover) with categories and subcategories; the Govern function added in 2.0 covers organizational policies, risk-management strategy, supply chain risk management, and roles and responsibilities including board-level oversight. The FFIEC IT Examination Handbook is the principal supervisory frame for examiner expectations on information security and cybersecurity. Fund advisers are not directly subject to FFIEC supervision but the framework appears in industry leading practice and in third-party-vendor cyber-attestation work.
- Link: https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework — NIST CSF 2.0 (February 2024); https://ithandbook.ffiec.gov/it-booklets/information-security/ — FFIEC IT Handbook Information Security booklet
- Notes: The cyber overlay for this skill leans on Reg S-P (May 2024) for the binding adviser/fund obligations and on NIST CSF 2.0 / FFIEC for substantive control framing.
