# Source anchors — best-execution-surveillance

Citations and excerpts for the named regulatory anchors this skill relies on. Loaded conditionally based on the registration class (investment adviser, broker-dealer, dual registrant, fund adviser) and the rails in scope. Verify section references and dates on the analysis date.

## Investment Advisers Act §206 — fiduciary duty

- **Citation**: Investment Advisers Act of 1940 §206 (15 USC 80b-6); 17 CFR 275.206 implementing rules.
- **What it carries**: General antifraud and fiduciary duty owed by an investment adviser to its clients. Best execution sits within the duty of care branch of the fiduciary duty; the Commission has restated the position across decades of guidance.
- **Operative for this skill**: Quarterly / annual best-execution review for an SEC- or state-registered investment adviser is the periodic execution of this duty. The duty is principles-based; the documentation expectation is what makes the duty examinable.
- **Link**: https://www.sec.gov/about/laws/iaa40.pdf
- **Section reference**: §206 (general); §206(3) for principal and agency-cross trade consent; §206(4) for fraud prevention rulemaking.

## SEC Commission Interpretation Regarding Standard of Conduct for Investment Advisers — Release IA-5248

- **Citation**: SEC Release No. IA-5248 (June 5, 2019), "Commission Interpretation Regarding Standard of Conduct for Investment Advisers."
- **What it carries**: Commission's restatement of the fiduciary duty an adviser owes its clients, including the duty of care components (suitability, monitoring, best execution).
- **Operative for this skill**: The release articulates best execution as a core component of duty of care. Cite this for the doctrinal anchor on the IA side; pair with the older 1986 IM Guidance position when historical context is in scope.
- **Link**: https://www.sec.gov/rules/interp/2019/ia-5248.pdf
- **Section reference**: Section II.A "Duty of Care" — best-execution discussion. [verify exact subsection on analysis date]

## Investment Company Act §17(e) — affiliated brokerage

- **Citation**: Investment Company Act of 1940 §17(e) (15 USC 80a-17(e)); 17 CFR 270.17e-1 implementing rule.
- **What it carries**: Restrictions on a registered investment company paying brokerage commissions to an affiliated broker. §17(e)(1) limits compensation to a "reasonable and fair" amount relative to comparable transactions; §17(e)(2) requires the fund's board to adopt procedures and to determine that compensation is consistent with the standard.
- **Operative for this skill**: Where the fund routes any portion of its brokerage to an affiliated broker, the best-execution review must produce the comparable-rate evidence supporting the §17(e)(1) attestation. The §17(e)(2) board procedures and the periodic determination feed the 15(c) materials.
- **Link**: https://www.sec.gov/about/laws/ica40.pdf
- **Section reference**: §17(e); Rule 17e-1.

## Exchange Act §28(e) — soft-dollar safe harbor

- **Citation**: Securities Exchange Act of 1934 §28(e) (15 USC 78bb(e)); SEC Interpretive Release 34-54165 (July 18, 2006), "Commission Guidance Regarding Client Commission Practices Under Section 28(e) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934."
- **What it carries**: Safe harbor for an adviser's good-faith determination that brokerage and research services received are reasonable in relation to commissions paid, notwithstanding the adviser's duty to seek best execution. The 2006 interpretive release defines eligible "brokerage and research services" and addresses mixed-use products and commission-sharing arrangements.
- **Operative for this skill**: The skill's soft-dollar review block cites §28(e) and the 2006 release for eligibility determinations. Mixed-use products require a reasonable allocation methodology between research-eligible and non-research portions and contemporaneous documentation.
- **Link**: https://www.sec.gov/rules/interp/2006/34-54165.pdf
- **Section reference**: 2006 release Section III "Eligibility Criteria"; Section IV "Mixed-Use Items"; Section V "Commission-Sharing Arrangements." [verify exact subsections on analysis date]

## FINRA Rule 5310 — Best Execution and Interpositioning

- **Citation**: FINRA Rule 5310, including Supplementary Material .01 through .09.
- **What it carries**: The broker-dealer best-execution obligation. .01 lists the factors the firm must consider when seeking the most favourable terms reasonably available (character of the market, size and type of transaction, number of markets checked, accessibility of the quotation, terms and conditions of the order). .09 sets the "regular and rigorous review" requirement: a firm that does not conduct an order-by-order best-execution review must conduct a regular and rigorous review of execution quality at the venue level.
- **Operative for this skill**: This is the broker-dealer side of the periodic review. .09 is the doctrinal hook for the venue-level surveillance; .01 is the factor list applied in the trade-by-trade exception sampling.
- **Link**: https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/5310
- **Section reference**: Rule 5310; Supplementary Material .01 (factors); .09 (regular and rigorous review).

## FINRA Regulatory Notice 15-46 — Best Execution Guidance

- **Citation**: FINRA Regulatory Notice 15-46 (November 2015), "Best Execution: Guidance on Best Execution Obligations in Equity, Options and Fixed Income Markets."
- **What it carries**: FINRA's restatement of the Rule 5310 obligation across asset classes. Specific guidance on equity routing in low-priced and thinly traded securities, options market structure, and the application of best-execution duties in fixed-income markets where the dealer-market structure shapes the analysis.
- **Operative for this skill**: Cite as the FINRA-level interpretive context for cross-asset-class application of the rule. For the fixed-income overlay, the notice is the reference for evaluating execution quality in markets without a consolidated tape.
- **Link**: https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/15-46
- **Section reference**: Whole notice. [verify whether superseded by 2024-2026 best-execution refresh on analysis date]

## FINRA best-execution refresh (post-2015)

- **Citation**: FINRA Regulatory Notice 15-46 remains the principal cross-asset-class best-execution notice. FINRA has from time to time issued additional Regulatory Notices, exam priorities letters, and Reports on Examination Findings touching best-execution practice; verify any post-2015 refresh applicable on the analysis date and pull it into the factor analysis only where it is genuinely operative. Do not assert a 2024-2026 best-execution refresh exists without confirming.
- **Operative for this skill**: Use 15-46 as the baseline. Where FINRA has published a more recent refresh applicable to the period under review, supplement; do not invent.

## FINRA Rule 3110 — Supervision

- **Citation**: FINRA Rule 3110.
- **What it carries**: Written supervisory procedures requirement. The firm must establish, maintain, and enforce written procedures reasonably designed to achieve compliance with applicable securities laws and FINRA rules, including best execution.
- **Operative for this skill**: The best-execution committee charter, the regular-and-rigorous review cadence, and the surveillance procedures all sit under the WSP framework Rule 3110 requires.
- **Link**: https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/3110
- **Section reference**: Rule 3110(a), (b).

## SEC Rule 605 — Market Center Disclosure of Execution Quality

- **Citation**: 17 CFR 242.605.
- **What it carries**: Monthly execution-quality reports from market centres for covered orders in National Market System stocks. Reports the effective spread, realised spread, price improvement, fill rate, and execution speed by order type and size category for each NMS security.
- **Operative for this skill**: The 605 disclosures from venues used by the firm are reconciled against the firm's own execution-quality measurements as a cross-check. Material divergence is a finding to investigate before relying on either source.
- **Link**: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-242/section-242.605
- **Section reference**: §242.605.

## SEC Rule 606 — Broker-Dealer Disclosure of Order Routing

- **Citation**: 17 CFR 242.606.
- **What it carries**: Quarterly order-routing reports broker-dealers must publish, plus the on-request customer-specific order-routing report under Rule 606(b)(3). Reports identify venues, percentage of customer orders routed, terms (rebates, fees, payment for order flow), and the relationships with venues.
- **Operative for this skill**: 606 is a routing-disclosure obligation; it does not by itself satisfy the Rule 5310 or §206 best-execution review. The skill reconciles the firm's own 606 figures against internal routing data and uses 606 as one input, not the conclusion.
- **Link**: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-242/section-242.606
- **Section reference**: §242.606(a) quarterly report; §242.606(b)(3) customer-specific request.

## SEC Final Rule on Order Execution Disclosure — Release 34-99679

- **Citation**: SEC Release No. 34-99679 (March 6, 2024), "Disclosure of Order Execution Information." Compliance date August 1, 2026 [verify against the most recent SEC compliance-date guidance — the SEC has historically issued staff guidance and short extensions on Rule 605 as the industry approaches the implementation date].
- **What it carries**: Amendments to Rule 605 expanding the universe of reporting entities (broker-dealers introducing more than 100,000 customer accounts in addition to market centres), adding new statistics, modernising reporting formats, and adjusting the order-size and type categories.
- **Operative for this skill**: For periods on or after the August 1, 2026 compliance date, the reconciliation block uses the amended definitions and categories. For earlier periods, the legacy 605 fields apply. The skill date-stamps the operative regime for the period under review.
- **Link**: https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2024/34-99679.pdf
- **Section reference**: Amendments to 17 CFR §242.605 and the compliance-date table.

## SEC Division of Examinations — Annual Examination Priorities

- **Citation**: SEC Division of Examinations annual priorities document, current cycle. [verify which cycle is current on the analysis date]
- **What it carries**: Current-year examination focus areas. Best-execution practices appear annually for advisers and broker-dealers; specific sub-themes (off-exchange routing, payment for order flow, fixed-income markups, fractional-share routing, options retail flow) shift cycle to cycle.
- **Operative for this skill**: The skill consults the current-cycle priorities to weight the surveillance focus areas and the committee-pack discussion. Older cycle priorities are not authoritative for the current year.

## Form N-CEN and Form N-CSR — fund disclosures

- **Citation**: Form N-CEN (annual census report by registered investment companies); Form N-CSR (certified shareholder report).
- **What it carries**: Form N-CEN includes brokerage and aggregate commission information for registered funds. Form N-CSR includes the certified shareholder report that may carry portfolio-management and trading discussion relevant to best-execution practices.
- **Operative for this skill**: For the fund-adviser variant, the surveillance review feeds disclosures appearing in these forms; consistency between the surveillance findings and the form disclosures is itself a review point.
- **Link**: https://www.sec.gov/forms (navigate to N-CEN and N-CSR)

## Investment Company Act §15(c) — fund board approval of advisory contract

- **Citation**: Investment Company Act §15(c) (15 USC 80a-15(c)).
- **What it carries**: Fund board's annual approval of the investment advisory contract requires the board to evaluate factors that include the adviser's brokerage allocation and best-execution practices. Practice has built around the "Gartenberg factors" (after Gartenberg v. Merrill Lynch Asset Management, 694 F.2d 923 (2d Cir. 1982)) for fee evaluation; brokerage and best execution sit alongside as a discrete topic in the materials.
- **Operative for this skill**: For the fund-adviser variant, the surveillance write-up is the factual record the adviser provides to the fund board for the §15(c) deliberations on brokerage and best execution.
- **Link**: https://www.sec.gov/about/laws/ica40.pdf
- **Section reference**: §15(c).

## Rule 38a-1 — fund compliance program

- **Citation**: 17 CFR 270.38a-1.
- **What it carries**: Fund's compliance program rule. Requires the fund to adopt and review written compliance policies and procedures reasonably designed to prevent violations of the federal securities laws, including those of its service providers (the adviser).
- **Operative for this skill**: The fund's CCO reports annually on the operation of the compliance program, including the adviser's best-execution program where the fund's brokerage runs through it.
- **Link**: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-270/section-270.38a-1
- **Section reference**: Rule 38a-1.
