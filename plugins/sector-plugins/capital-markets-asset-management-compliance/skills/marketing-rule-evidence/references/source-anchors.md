## Primary anchors

### 1. Investment Advisers Act §206 — antifraud foundation; §206(4) rulemaking authority

- Citation: Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Section 206, 15 U.S.C. §80b-6, particularly §206(1) and §206(2) (fraud, deceit, manipulative practices) and §206(4) (rulemaking authority defining and prescribing means reasonably designed to prevent fraudulent, deceptive, or manipulative acts).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: §206 imposes the federal antifraud standard on registered investment advisers. The general antifraud reach informs every Marketing Rule provision: a representation that is technically compliant with the disclosure mechanics of Rule 206(4)-1 can still violate §206(1) / (2) where it is fraudulent or deceptive in context. §206(4) is the rulemaking authority under which Rule 206(4)-1 (Marketing Rule) and Rule 206(4)-7 (Compliance Programs) are issued.
- Link: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/80b-6 — §206(1) fraud or deceit; §206(2) transactions that operate as a fraud or deceit; §206(4) rulemaking authority
- Notes: Statutory anchor for the antifraud / fair-and-balanced overlay that runs through every Marketing Rule analysis.

### 2. SEC Rule 206(4)-1 — Marketing Rule (as adopted Dec 22 2020; compliance date Nov 4 2022)

- Citation: 17 CFR §275.206(4)-1, "Investment Adviser Marketing", adopted by SEC Release No. IA-5653 (December 22, 2020), with a compliance date of November 4, 2022.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The Marketing Rule sets advertising and solicitation requirements for SEC-registered investment advisers under the §206(4) antifraud framework. Paragraph (a) sets definitions of "advertisement" (the two-prong definition: the general direct-or-indirect-communication-offering-advisory-services prong, and the compensated testimonial / endorsement prong), with carve-outs (one-on-one communications excluded except for hypothetical performance; extemporaneous oral communications excluded; communications including hypothetical performance always covered; certain regulatory communications excluded). Paragraph (b) sets the seven general prohibitions (untrue statement; unsubstantiated material claim; untrue or misleading implication or inference; reference to specific investment advice that is not fair and balanced; not including or excluding performance results in a manner that is not fair and balanced; otherwise materially misleading). Paragraph (c) sets testimonials and endorsements requirements (required disclosures of compensation status, client status, and material conflicts; oversight; written agreement where compensation exceeds the prescribed threshold; ineligible-person disqualification keyed to the named statutory categories). Paragraph (d) sets third-party-rating requirements (reasonable basis; required disclosures of date, period covered, identity, compensation). Paragraph (d)(6) sets hypothetical-performance pre-conditions (policies and procedures designed to ensure relevance to the likely audience; sufficient information for the audience to understand the criteria and assumptions; sufficient information for the audience to understand the risks and limitations). Paragraph (e) sets prohibited and required performance-presentation mechanics (gross presented with net of equal prominence and method, with a narrow one-on-one private-fund-investor exception; required 1-, 5-, 10-year periods unless the portfolio's life is shorter; predecessor-performance portability conditions; related-performance composite construction; extracted-performance disclosure mechanics).
- Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-275/section-275.206(4)-1 — §275.206(4)-1(a) definitions; (b) general prohibitions; (c) testimonials and endorsements; (d) third-party ratings; (d)(6) hypothetical performance; (e) performance-presentation mechanics
- Notes: Operative anchor for the per-statement-type classification, the disclosures matrix, the hypothetical pre-conditions, and the testimonial / endorsement and third-party-rating checklists. Citations to specific paragraphs run through every section of the pack.

### 3. SEC Marketing Rule Adopting Release IA-5653 (December 22, 2020) — preamble interpretive guidance

- Citation: U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, "Investment Adviser Marketing", Final Rule, Release Nos. IA-5653; File No. S7-21-19, 86 Fed. Reg. 13024 (March 5, 2021), adopted December 22, 2020.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The adopting release is the Commission's interpretive guidance on the rule text. It explains the two-prong definition of "advertisement", the carve-outs and their boundaries, the general prohibitions and how they operate against the antifraud backdrop, the testimonial / endorsement framework and the rationale for retiring the prior cash-solicitation rule, the third-party-rating mechanics, the hypothetical-performance treatment (and why the (d)(6) conditions apply regardless of recipient count), and the performance-presentation mechanics including the gross-and-net pairing rationale and the narrow private-fund-investor one-on-one exception.
- Link: https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2020/ia-5653.pdf — Adopting Release, "Definitions" section; "General Prohibitions" section; "Testimonials and Endorsements" section; "Third-Party Ratings" section; "Performance" section
- Notes: Where the rule text alone is ambiguous on a marginal classification (one-on-one carve-out boundary; what counts as "extemporaneous"; predecessor-portability conditions), the adopting release is the named interpretive source.

### 4. SEC Division of Examinations Risk Alert, "Examinations Focused on the New Investment Adviser Marketing Rule" (September 19, 2022)

- Citation: U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, Division of Examinations, "Risk Alert: Examinations Focused on the New Investment Adviser Marketing Rule", September 19, 2022.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The first Marketing Rule risk alert sets out EXAMS' initial review areas for the new rule, with emphasis on (i) the marketing-rule policies and procedures required under Rule 206(4)-7 (the compliance program rule) tailored to the new Marketing Rule; (ii) substantive compliance with the general prohibitions; (iii) substantiation requirements for material statements of fact; (iv) performance advertising requirements; and (v) the books-and-records amendments to Rule 204-2.
- Link: https://www.sec.gov/files/exams-risk-alert-marketing-rule.pdf — Risk Alert, "Initial Areas of Review" section
- Notes: Pin point for the EXAMS-focus posture in pre-dissemination and look-back reviews. Where the firm is in or near an exam, the pack cross-references the alert's named focus areas in the residual-issues block.

### 5. SEC Division of Examinations Risk Alert, "Examinations Focused on Additional Areas of the Adviser Marketing Rule" (June 8, 2023)

- Citation: U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, Division of Examinations, "Risk Alert: Examinations Focused on Additional Areas of the Adviser Marketing Rule", June 8, 2023.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The second Marketing Rule risk alert expands the EXAMS focus areas with emphasis on (i) Marketing Rule books-and-records and substantiation; (ii) the Marketing Rule's general prohibitions; (iii) testimonial and endorsement compliance, including the disclosure-clarity and clear-and-prominent placement requirements, written-agreement requirements, oversight, and ineligible-person disqualification; (iv) third-party-rating compliance, including the reasonable-basis and the rating-related disclosures; and (v) Form ADV amendments tied to the Marketing Rule (Item 5.L. responses on the use of testimonials, endorsements, third-party ratings, and the inclusion of hypothetical or predecessor performance in advertising).

### 7. SEC Rule 204-2 — Books and records (Marketing Rule recordkeeping)

- Citation: 17 CFR §275.204-2, "Books and records to be maintained by investment advisers", particularly §275.204-2(a)(11) (advertisements and recommendations to ten or more persons; records sufficient to demonstrate the calculation of any performance shown), §275.204-2(a)(15) (testimonials, endorsements, and third-party-rating records, including written agreements, materials supporting the testimonial / endorsement, the ineligible-person determination), and §275.204-2(a)(16) (records supporting performance claims, including all accounts forming the basis of any performance).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: §204-2(a)(11) requires the adviser to retain originals of all written communications received and copies of all written communications sent regarding any recommendation made or proposed to be made or any advice given or proposed to be given, and any advertisement; the rule was amended to harmonize with the Marketing Rule's expanded definition of advertisement, including records sufficient to demonstrate the calculations of any performance or rate of return in any advertisement. §204-2(a)(15) sets the records adviser must retain related to any testimonial, endorsement, or third-party rating used in advertising, including the testimonial or endorsement itself, the written agreement (where required), the ineligible-person determination, and any compensation paid. §204-2(a)(16) sets the records adviser must retain to support performance claims, including all accounts that the adviser includes in any composite or that form the basis of any extracted, related, or model performance.
- Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-275/section-275.204-2 — §275.204-2(a)(11) advertisement and performance-calculation records; (a)(15) testimonial / endorsement / third-party-rating records; (a)(16) performance-supporting records
- Notes: Operative anchor for the books-and-records evidence index. Records "sufficient to demonstrate" includes the worksheet, the underlying-account trail, the methodology pin, and the date-stamped attestations.

### 8. SEC Rule 206(4)-7 — Compliance Programs of Investment Advisers (the policy-and-procedures tie-in)

- Citation: 17 CFR §275.206(4)-7, "Compliance procedures and practices", particularly the requirement for written policies and procedures reasonably designed to prevent violations of the Advisers Act and the rules thereunder, the annual review requirement, and the chief-compliance-officer designation.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Each registered investment adviser must adopt and implement written policies and procedures reasonably designed to prevent violation, by the adviser and its supervised persons, of the Advisers Act and the rules thereunder. The adviser must review the adequacy of those policies and procedures and the effectiveness of their implementation no less frequently than annually. The adviser must designate a chief compliance officer responsible for administering the compliance policies and procedures.
- Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-275/section-275.206(4)-7 — §275.206(4)-7 written policies and procedures; annual review; CCO designation
- Notes: The Marketing Rule's hypothetical-performance pre-conditions reference the firm's policies and procedures; the pack pins the audience-appropriateness policy under this rule. Annual look-back reviews on advertising are run under this rule.

### 9. FINRA Rule 2210 — Communications with the public (boundary anchor for dual registrants and BD-only marketing)

- Citation: FINRA Rule 2210, "Communications with the Public", particularly the categorisation framework (institutional communication; retail communication; correspondence), the principal pre-use approval requirements, the recordkeeping requirements, and the content standards.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: FINRA Rule 2210 sets the communications-with-the-public framework for FINRA-member broker-dealers. Categorisation drives pre-use approval, principal sign-off, filing, and recordkeeping requirements. Content standards include the prohibitions on false, exaggerated, unwarranted, promissory, or misleading statements or claims; on omitting material information; and on predicting or projecting performance (subject to specified exceptions). For dual registrants (BD-IA), some communications fall under both Rule 2210 and Rule 206(4)-1; the firm's dual-track review allocates accordingly. For broker-dealer-only entities, Rule 2210 is the operative framework, not the Marketing Rule.
- Link: https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2210 — FINRA Rule 2210, particularly (a) definitions; (b) approval, review, and recordkeeping; (c) filing requirements and review procedures; (d) content standards
- Notes: Boundary anchor. The pack cites Rule 2210 to flag the dual-registrant or BD-only routing decision; the pack does not produce a Rule 2210 review (that work routes to the broker-dealer compliance function).

### 10. SEC Rule 482 and Rule 156 — Registered fund advertising (boundary anchor)

- Citation: Securities Act Rule 482, 17 CFR §230.482 (advertising by an investment company); Section 24(b) of the Investment Company Act; SEC Statement on Sales Literature, Rule 156 under the Securities Act, 17 CFR §230.156 (factors relevant to whether sales literature is materially misleading).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Registered-investment-company advertising and supplemental sales literature operate under a separate framework: Rule 482 sets the form, content, and standardised-performance requirements for registered fund advertisements; Rule 156 sets the factors for whether a sales-literature representation is materially misleading. Where the advertiser is the registered fund (not the adviser as adviser), the routing is Rule 482 / Rule 156, not the Marketing Rule. Where the advertiser is the adviser advertising its advisory services to the fund or to other clients, the Marketing Rule governs.
- Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-230/section-230.482 — Rule 482 fund advertising; https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-230/section-230.156 — Rule 156 sales literature
- Notes: Boundary anchor. The pack flags the routing decision; registered-fund advertisement review routes to the fund's marketing-review function and the fund-board oversight (see `fund-board-reporting`).
