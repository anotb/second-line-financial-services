# Cross-cutting overlay: privacy — regulatory-impact-assessment

Load this overlay when the rule under assessment touches consumer-financial-data privacy, GLBA Privacy Rule, GLBA Safeguards Rule, state privacy laws (CCPA / CPRA, Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Utah, others), HIPAA, sectoral data-protection rules (children's data, health data, biometric data), or cross-border data-transfer mechanisms. Cross-cutting overlays do not change the assessment's spine; they tighten the source-anchor list and the named-policy and named-control patterns the assessment reads against.

## Anchored privacy sources

- **GLBA Privacy Rule** — 16 CFR Part 313 (FTC; for non-bank consumer-finance providers); 12 CFR Part 1016 (CFPB; for institutions under CFPB authority); 12 CFR Part 332 (FDIC; for state non-member banks); 12 CFR Part 40 (OCC; for national banks); 12 CFR Part 216 (FRB; Reg P, for state member banks and BHCs) [verify subpart and section per institution]. https://www.ecfr.gov/ .
- **GLBA Safeguards Rule** — 16 CFR Part 314 (FTC); 12 CFR Part 30 Appendix B for federally supervised banks [verify subsection]. https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/safeguards-rule .
- **SEC Reg S-P** — 17 CFR Part 248 (Privacy of Consumer Financial Information and Safeguarding Personal Information). 2024 amendments [verify final-rule release number, section, and effective date]. https://www.sec.gov/rules/final.shtml .
- **California Consumer Privacy Act / California Privacy Rights Act** — Cal. Civ. Code §§1798.100-1798.199.100 [verify section]; California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) regulations under 11 CCR §7000 [verify section]. https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa .
- **State comprehensive privacy laws** — Virginia (CDPA), Colorado (CPA), Connecticut (CTDPA), Utah (UCPA), Texas (TDPSA), Oregon (OCPA), Montana (MCDPA), and others as enacted [verify state-specific citation, effective date, and exemptions for GLBA-covered data]. https://iapp.org/resources/article/us-state-privacy-legislation-tracker/ .
- **HIPAA Privacy Rule and Security Rule** — 45 CFR Parts 160, 162, 164 [verify subpart and section]. https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/index.html .
- **Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA)** — 16 CFR Part 312 [verify subsection]. https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule-coppa .
- **Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)** — 15 USC §1681; Reg V at 12 CFR Part 1022 [verify subsection]. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1022 .
- **CFPB Section 1033 Personal Financial Data Rights Rule** — 12 CFR Part 1033 [verify final-rule citation, subsection, and effective date]. https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/final-rules/required-rulemaking-on-personal-financial-data-rights/ .
- **State biometric privacy laws** — Illinois BIPA (740 ILCS 14); Texas CUBI; Washington biometric law; New York City biometric law [verify citation per state]. https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=3004 .
- **EU GDPR** for firms with EU operations — Regulation (EU) 2016/679, articles as relevant. https://gdpr-info.eu/ .
- **Cross-border data-transfer mechanisms** — EU-US Data Privacy Framework (DPF); Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs); UK IDTA / Addendum [verify each instrument's effective version]. https://www.dataprivacyframework.gov/ .

## Named-policy and named-control patterns the rule may touch

When the rule is privacy-anchored, the impact assessment's policy-impact section typically references one or more:

- **Privacy Programme and Policy** (privacy notice; data-collection-and-use policy; consent-management policy; opt-out-and-opt-in framework; subject-rights-handling policy).
- **Data Inventory and Mapping Programme** (data-element inventory; system-of-record mapping; data-flow mapping; cross-border-transfer mapping).
- **Subject Rights Programme** (access, deletion, correction, portability, opt-out-of-sale, opt-out-of-targeted-advertising; under CCPA/CPRA, the consumer has the right to know, the right to delete, the right to correct, the right to opt-out, the right to limit use of sensitive personal information, and the right to non-discrimination).
- **Consent-Management Programme** (granular consent; consent-revocation; consent-record retention; under CCPA/CPRA, the global privacy control GPC).
- **Data Retention and Disposal Programme** (retention-period schedule; secure disposal; record-of-processing).
- **Data Sharing and Third-Party Programme** (vendor data-handling expectations; service-provider contracts under CCPA; processor agreements under EU GDPR; data-protection-impact-assessment process).
- **Cross-Border Data-Transfer Programme** (standard contractual clauses; data-privacy-framework certification; UK addendum; transfer impact assessment).
- **Adverse-Action and Reason-Code Programme** (under FCRA / Reg B for credit and insurance; adverse-action notice content).
- **Identity-Theft Red Flags Programme** under 16 CFR Part 681 [verify subsection].
- **Children's Data Programme** (where applicable) under COPPA.
- **HIPAA Privacy and Security Programme** (where applicable) under 45 CFR Parts 160, 164.
- **Section 1033 Open Banking Programme** (data-access portal; consumer-controlled-data-sharing; third-party-data-recipient verification; revocation handling).

## Reporting-and-disclosure-impact patterns

Privacy rules routinely change the reporting-and-disclosure surface:

- **Public disclosures**: privacy notice (GLBA Privacy Rule annual delivery); CCPA notice at collection; CCPA notice at sale or sharing; opt-out-of-sale or opt-out-of-targeted-advertising disclosures; HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices.
- **Regulator notifications**: state breach-notification under each state's breach-notification law (timing and content vary by state); GLBA Safeguards Rule notification (FTC); SEC Reg S-P notification (2024 amendment) [verify final-rule timing]; HHS-CMS HIPAA breach notification (60 days, with state-by-state additional requirements); CFPB consumer-notification posture for material privacy events (typically driven by enforcement convention rather than rule).
- **Subject-rights reporting**: CCPA requires firms over a transaction-volume threshold to publish metrics on subject-rights-request volume and response timing in their privacy notice [verify CCPA threshold and metric content].
- **Board reporting**: GLBA Safeguards Rule (16 CFR Part 314) [verify section] requires written reporting to the board or governing body at least annually; state insurance data security model law parallel.

## Strategy-lens patterns specific to privacy rules

The privacy strategy lens has identifiable patterns:

- **Industry-association alignment**: IAPP for cross-industry privacy engagement; the State Privacy and Security Coalition (SPSC) for state-comprehensive-privacy-law engagement; trade-association privacy committees (BPI, ABA, SIFMA, ICI, IAA, ACLI, FTA, AFC) for sector-specific engagement.
- **State-by-state engagement**: state attorney general offices (CCPA / CPRA enforcement is by California Attorney General and the California Privacy Protection Agency); Connecticut, Colorado, Virginia, Utah AGs; the multistate AG coalitions on specific topics.
- **Federal engagement**: CFPB on Section 1033 and on consumer-financial-data rules; FTC on GLBA Safeguards updates and on broader privacy themes; SEC on Reg S-P updates and on adviser-and-fund-side privacy; HHS-CMS on HIPAA topics; OFAC where sanctions data-handling intersects.
- **Cross-border posture**: where the firm operates internationally, EU DPAs and UK ICO are parallel-engagement points. The DPF, the EU adequacy decision, and SCCs are operative cross-border mechanisms.
- **Examiner-posture-shift signal**: CFPB Director speeches on consumer-financial-data; FTC enforcement pattern on GLBA Safeguards (recent enforcement actions against motor-vehicle dealers, mortgage companies, and others have signalled emphasis); state-AG enforcement under CCPA/CPRA; HHS-CMS breach-portal trend pattern.
- **Cross-rule coordination**: privacy rules routinely overlap (a CCPA/CPRA rule update plus a GLBA Privacy Rule update plus a Section 1033 rule update). The impact assessment names the cross-rule coordination needs (e.g., a single privacy notice that satisfies multiple regimes; a single data-inventory that supports multiple subject-rights regimes).

## When the privacy overlay applies in part vs full

Load the overlay in full when the rule's primary subject is consumer-financial-data privacy, GLBA Privacy or Safeguards Rule, state privacy law, HIPAA, sectoral privacy (children, health, biometric), or cross-border data-transfer mechanisms. Load the overlay in part (only the relevant sections above) when the rule has a privacy dimension among other dimensions (e.g., a marketing-rule update with a consumer-data-handling sub-section). Do not load the overlay when the rule is GLBA Safeguards Rule technical-controls primary (load the cyber overlay instead, which carries the technical-controls anchor).
