# Cross-cutting overlay: climate — regulatory-impact-assessment

Load this overlay when the rule under assessment touches climate-related financial risk, climate disclosure, the federal banking agencies' climate-related principles for large banks, NAIC climate risk frameworks for insurers, SEC climate-disclosure rules (where in force), or international frameworks (TCFD, ISSB, EU CSRD) that apply to the firm's reporting perimeter. Cross-cutting overlays do not change the assessment's spine; they tighten the source-anchor list and the named-policy and named-control patterns the assessment reads against.

## Named-policy and named-control patterns the rule may touch

When the rule is climate-anchored, the impact assessment's policy-impact section typically references one or more:

- **Climate Risk Governance and Strategy Programme** (board oversight of climate risk; senior-management responsibility; integration of climate risk into strategy and capital-and-liquidity planning).
- **Climate Risk Management Programme** (identification, measurement, monitoring, and control of physical and transition climate risks across the risk taxonomy: credit, market, liquidity, operational, legal, compliance, strategic, reputational).
- **Scenario-Analysis Programme** (climate scenario analysis with defined scenarios, time horizons, and use cases; the FRB Pilot Climate Scenario Analysis exercise patterns).
- **Climate-related Data Programme** (asset-level GHG-emissions data; physical-risk-asset-location data; transition-risk-counterparty data).
- **Climate Disclosure Programme** (Reg S-K Items 1500-1508 disclosures where in force; Reg S-X Article 14 financial-statement-level disclosures; NAIC Climate Risk Disclosure Survey response; CDP / TCFD / ISSB voluntary disclosure where elected).
- **Climate Risk Reporting Programme** (board reporting on climate risk; supervisory reporting where applicable; investor reporting).
- **Climate Risk Third-Party Programme** (third-party assessment of climate-related disclosure-data providers, scenario-analysis platforms, GHG-accounting platforms).
- **Insurance Underwriting Climate Programme** (where applicable, climate-related underwriting risk consideration; rate-and-form-filing climate justification under state insurance codes).

## Reporting-and-disclosure-impact patterns

Climate rules routinely change the reporting-and-disclosure surface:

- **Public disclosures**: SEC Form 10-K Reg S-K Item 1500 disclosures (where in force); Reg S-X Article 14 financial-statement-level disclosures; voluntary TCFD or ISSB-aligned reports; CDP submissions.
- **Regulator filings**: NAIC Climate Risk Disclosure Survey response (state-by-state filings); California SB 253 GHG-emissions reporting and SB 261 climate-related-financial-risk reporting (where applicable); FIO data calls; EU CSRD reporting (where applicable).
- **Board reporting**: federal banking principles call for board oversight of climate risk and senior-management accountability; the firm's climate-related-financial-risk MIS feeds the board.
- **Examiner expectations**: federal banking agencies' principles drive examination-priority emphasis for $100B+ banks; NYDFS climate-risk-management framework drives state-supervisor examination posture for licensed insurers.

## Strategy-lens patterns specific to climate rules

The climate strategy lens has identifiable patterns:

- **Industry-association alignment**: BPI climate working group; ABA climate working group; ACLI climate working group; SIFMA climate engagement; ICI fund-side climate engagement; the Risk Management Association climate-risk subcommittee; the Network for Greening the Financial System (NGFS) for central-bank-and-supervisor cross-jurisdictional engagement.
- **Litigation posture**: SEC climate-disclosure rule litigation has affected effective-date posture and the firm's compliance timeline. The assessment names the litigation status and the firm's read of the litigation outcome's implication for the compliance pathway.
- **Cross-jurisdictional coordination**: where the firm reports under SEC climate-disclosure (where in force), CSRD (EU), ISSB (where mandated by jurisdictions), California SB 253 / SB 261, and NAIC Climate Risk Disclosure Survey, the firm's reporting consolidation reduces duplicative-data burden. The impact assessment names the consolidation strategy.
- **Examiner-posture-shift signal**: federal banking agencies' interagency principles; FRB Pilot Climate Scenario Analysis communications; NYDFS Industry Letters; California DOI climate-related circulars; international-supervisor communications (ECB, BoE, MAS, HKMA, JFSA).
- **Stakeholder posture**: investor pressure (asset-manager net-zero commitments; proxy-advisory climate-related vote pattern); customer pressure (corporate-customer net-zero commitments flowing into supplier expectations); employee and community-stakeholder posture.
- **Escalation triggers**: For federally supervised banks at $100B+ in assets, the federal banking principles drive board-level engagement on climate-risk-management programme. For SEC registrants, the climate-disclosure rule (where in force) drives audit-committee and disclosure-committee engagement. For state-regulated insurers in mandatory-disclosure states, the climate-survey response is a chief-risk-officer or general-counsel-led artifact.

## When the climate overlay applies in part vs full

Load the overlay in full when the rule's primary subject is climate-related financial risk, climate disclosure, climate-related-financial-risk management framework, or climate-related insurance underwriting. Load the overlay in part when the rule has a climate dimension among other dimensions (e.g., a broader sustainability or ESG rule with a climate-component sub-section). Do not load the overlay when the rule is sustainability-related but climate-tangential (the climate overlay is climate-specific; broader-ESG rules sit outside the spine of this overlay).
