# Source anchors: policy-diff

This file holds the named, dated regulatory and professional-standard sources the policy-diff skill anchors to. The SKILL.md file does not restate these citations beyond the activation-tuned description's "Anchored to:" line. Firm-specific anchors (a firm's own policy lifecycle, named approver titles, committee charters, policy-management system) belong in `references/firm-overlay.md`.

`[verify section]` placeholders mark anchors where the precise section reference still needs verification against the current edition of the source. They are deliberate, not omissions.

## Written-framework expectations (the why-policies-have-a-lifecycle anchor)

### 12 CFR Part 30 Appendix D — OCC Heightened Standards (effective September 11, 2014; amended 2024 to refresh references)

Anchor: §II (risk governance framework requirements), §III (board responsibilities for the framework), §V (policies, procedures, and standards), §VI (independent risk management). [verify the exact lettered subdivisions; the appendix uses Roman numerals with lettered sub-paragraphs.]

What this skill relies on: that large national banks operate under a written-framework expectation where policy changes are documented, attested, and routed to the board or a delegated committee. Policy diffs at heightened-standards banks therefore default to a routing posture that includes board or committee attestation for material changes.

- Link with section: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-I/part-30/appendix-Appendix%20D — §II framework, §V policies and procedures.

### OCC Bulletin 2021-36 — Governance over Policies, Procedures, and Other Written Guidance (issued September 27, 2021)

Anchor: Sections on the lifecycle expectation for written guidance — drafting, approval, communication, training, exception management, periodic review. [verify section labels against the published bulletin.]

What this skill relies on: that the OCC views the written-guidance lifecycle as a supervisory-relevant control. The diff is a lifecycle artifact; the materiality call, the approver routing, and the training-impact field map to the bulletin's lifecycle expectations.

- Link with section: https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2021/bulletin-2021-36.html — Bulletin §II, §III [verify section letters].

## Internal-control framing for policy-as-control

### COSO Internal Control — Integrated Framework (May 2013)

Anchor: Principle 10 (control activities — selection and development), Principle 12 (deployment through policies and procedures), Principle 13 (information and communication), Principle 16 (ongoing and separate evaluations). The framework treats policies and procedures as a deployment mechanism for control activities; the diff is the artifact that makes the deployment legible.

What this skill relies on: that policies are control activities in COSO terms; that revisions to a policy land on the control matrix because the deployment of the control changed; that the diff's downstream-impact-on-controls field is itself a Principle-12 expectation.

- Link with section: https://www.coso.org/internal-control — Principles 10, 12, 13, 16.

## Risk-reporting overlay

### BCBS 239 — Principles for Effective Risk Data Aggregation and Risk Reporting (Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, January 2013)

Anchor: Principle 11 (distribution and reporting frequency), Principle 13 (governance frameworks supporting risk reporting). Where the policy in scope supports risk reporting — a risk-appetite policy, a top-risks policy, a KRI escalation policy — BCBS 239 applies.

What this skill relies on: that diffs against risk-reporting-supporting policies carry frequency, distribution, and accuracy as named applicability dimensions; that material changes to a risk-reporting policy touch the risk committee at minimum.

- Link with section: https://www.bis.org/publ/bcbs239.htm — Principles 11, 13.

## Model-risk policy lifecycle

### SR 11-7 / OCC Bulletin 2011-12 — Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management (Federal Reserve and OCC, April 4, 2011)

Anchor: §VI (governance, policies, controls — including the policy on model risk management itself; review cadence; named-role expectations). [verify section labels against the published letter.]

What this skill relies on: that diffs of model-risk policies carry §VI's named-role and review-cadence expectations; that material changes to a model-risk policy almost always route to the model risk committee or equivalent and trigger a refresh of the model inventory's policy-version field.

- Link with section: https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1107.htm — §VI governance.

### OCC Bulletin 2026-13 — Revised Interagency Model Risk Management Guidance (2026)

Anchor: §III (refreshed control environment, third-party model controls, AI-system extensions). [verify section labels against the published bulletin.]

What this skill relies on: that diffs of model-risk policies post-2026 carry the OCC Bulletin 2026-13 refresh; that AI-system policy provisions are new sections in version B for many firms and carry material materiality calls.

- Link with section: https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/ — OCC Bulletin 2026-13 §III.

## Compliance-program written-policy lifecycle

### SEC Investment Advisers Act Rule 206(4)-7 — Compliance Programs of Investment Advisers (17 CFR §275.206(4)-7)

Anchor: §275.206(4)-7(a) (written policies and procedures requirement), §275.206(4)-7(b) (annual review requirement), §275.206(4)-7(c) (chief compliance officer designation).

What this skill relies on: that registered advisers must review their compliance policies at least annually; that the diff is the natural artifact of that annual review; that material changes route to the CCO and that the annual-review documentation is a supervisory artifact.

- Link with section: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-275/section-275.206(4)-7 — §275.206(4)-7(a) and (b).

### FINRA Rule 3110 — Supervision (effective December 1, 2014)

Anchor: Rule 3110(b) (written supervisory procedures), Rule 3110(b)(7) (review of WSPs), Supplementary Material .04 (annual certification of compliance and supervisory policies). [verify subparagraph labels against the FINRA online manual.]

What this skill relies on: that broker-dealer WSPs require written form, periodic review, and CEO annual certification; that diffs against WSPs are visible to FINRA on examination; that material changes route to the supervisory principal and to the annual-certification record.

- Link with section: https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/3110 — Rule 3110(b), Supplementary Material .04.

### Investment Company Act Rule 38a-1 — Compliance Programs of Registered Investment Companies (17 CFR §270.38a-1)

Anchor: §270.38a-1(a)(1) (written policies and procedures), §270.38a-1(a)(3) (annual review), §270.38a-1(b) (board approval and CCO designation).

What this skill relies on: that registered fund compliance policies require board approval; that diffs against fund compliance policies route to the board's compliance committee at minimum; that the annual-review obligation is a regulator-tested artifact.

- Link with section: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-270/section-270.38a-1 — §270.38a-1(a), (b).

## State cybersecurity-policy lifecycle

### NYDFS 23 NYCRR Part 500 — Cybersecurity Requirements for Financial Services Companies (effective March 1, 2017; amended November 1, 2023)

Anchor: §500.3 (cybersecurity policy approved at least annually by senior officer or board), §500.4 (chief information security officer; CISO reports), §500.17 (notice of cybersecurity event). [verify the post-November-2023 amendment numbering.]

What this skill relies on: that NYDFS-covered entities must approve their cybersecurity policy at least annually at senior-officer or board level; that the diff supports the §500.3 annual-approval record; that material changes that touch §500.11 third-party security policy ripple into the third-party policy.

- Link with section: https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance/cybersecurity — §500.3, §500.4, §500.11, §500.17.

## Third-party policy lifecycle

### Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management (OCC / FRB / FDIC, June 6, 2023)

Anchor: §III.A (planning), §III.B (due diligence and selection), §III.C (contract negotiation), §III.D (ongoing monitoring), §III.E (termination). The guidance frames third-party policy expectations across the lifecycle; diffs of TPRM policies map to the lifecycle phases.

What this skill relies on: that material changes to a TPRM policy almost always touch one or more lifecycle phases and ripple to the contract-template inventory and the vendor-onboarding workflow; that diffs against the 2023 guidance baseline are common in the post-issuance period.

- Link with section: https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2304.htm — §III.A through §III.E.

## Consumer-compliance management overlay

### CFPB Compliance Management System guidance — Supervision and Examination Manual (current edition)

Anchor: The CMS chapter framing the four CMS pillars: board and management oversight; compliance program (policies and procedures, training, monitoring and corrective action); consumer complaint response; compliance audit. [verify chapter and section labels against the current edition.]

What this skill relies on: that CFPB-supervised firms manage policy changes through the CMS's policies-and-procedures pillar; that the change-control record is a supervisory artifact; that material policy changes touch training and monitoring as part of the same CMS pillar.

- Link with section: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/supervision-examinations/ — CMS chapter, four pillars [verify pillar labels].

## EU operational-resilience overlay

### DORA — Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 on Digital Operational Resilience for the Financial Sector

Anchor: Articles 5-6 (governance and ICT risk management framework), Article 6(8) (review of the ICT risk management framework at least annually and following major ICT incidents), Article 28 (ICT third-party risk policy). [verify article numbering against the published OJ text.]

What this skill relies on: that DORA-covered entities review their ICT risk management framework annually; that the diff supports the Article 6(8) review; that the ICT third-party policy and the register of information are downstream artifacts of the policy diff for ICT-anchored policies.

- Link with section: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2554/oj — Articles 5, 6, 28.

## Information and communication discipline

### COSO Internal Control — Integrated Framework, Information and Communication component (May 2013)

Anchor: Principle 14 (internal communication), Principle 15 (external communication). Used here for the diff's communication-plan summary block — material policy changes need internal and where applicable external communication; the framework treats this as a control activity.

What this skill relies on: that the diff's communication-plan summary maps to Principles 14 and 15; that audience tagging (all-staff, line-managers, customer-facing, vendor-facing) is a Principle-14 deployment artifact.

- Link with section: https://www.coso.org/internal-control — Principles 14, 15.
