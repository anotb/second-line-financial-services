# Cross-cutting overlay — privacy

Loaded when the engagement scope flags privacy. Implementation plans triggered by or touching privacy rules carry distinct data-mapping, consent, and consumer-rights patterns.

## GLBA Safeguards Rule remediation

For financial institutions under FTC jurisdiction, the FTC Safeguards Rule (16 CFR Part 314, as amended December 2021 with subsequent amendments) drives the information-security programme requirements. Sections relevant to implementation plans include:

- §314.4(a) qualified individual designation;
- §314.4(b) risk assessment;
- §314.4(c) safeguards (access controls, encryption, MFA, logging, training, third-party oversight);
- §314.4(d) testing and monitoring;
- §314.4(e) training;
- §314.4(f) service-provider oversight;
- §314.4(h) incident-response plan;
- §314.4(i) annual report to the board or governing body;
- §314.5 notification (effective 2024).

For banks under Regulation P (12 CFR Part 1016) and the interagency Information Security Guidelines (12 CFR Part 30 Appendix B for OCC; FRB equivalents), the framework is parallel. The plan names the rule the entity falls under.

## Consumer-privacy overlay (state and federal)

Implementation plans touching state consumer-privacy laws (CCPA/CPRA, VCDPA, CPA, CTDPA, UCPA, IDPA, OCPA, MCDPA, TIPA, MTCDPA, others as adopted) integrate with the firm's consumer-privacy programme. Common workstream elements:

- data-mapping for in-scope personal information categories;
- consumer-rights workflow (right to know, right to delete, right to correct, right to opt out of sale or sharing, right to limit sensitive personal information, right to opt out of profiling);
- consumer-facing privacy notice updates;
- vendor and service-provider contractual updates (Data Processing Agreements);
- training and recordkeeping;
- annual reporting where required (CPRA cybersecurity audit and risk assessment under California regulation).

Federal-level consumer-financial-data rules (CFPB Section 1033 Personal Financial Data Rights rule, October 2024 final) bring an obligation set adjacent to but distinct from state privacy laws; the plan names which obligations land where.

## EU GDPR overlay where applicable

For EU-touching firms, GDPR Articles 5-22 (principles, lawful basis, data-subject rights), Articles 24-43 (controller and processor obligations), Articles 33-34 (notification), and Articles 44-50 (international transfers) frame the obligations. Schrems II and the EU-US Data Privacy Framework drive transfer mechanism choices. The plan integrates with the firm's GDPR programme rather than running parallel.

## Workstream and milestone shape

Typical privacy workstream pattern:

- data-mapping update reflecting the new obligation set;
- system changes for consumer-rights workflow (intake, identity verification, fulfilment, recordkeeping);
- privacy notice and disclosure updates;
- vendor / processor contract amendment cycle;
- training across the in-scope first-line population;
- testing and monitoring against the new requirement;
- annual reporting build (where the rule requires).

## Evidence patterns

Privacy acceptance criteria emphasise data-subject-rights fulfilment SLAs (e.g., CCPA 45-day right-to-know window with 45-day extension), consent records, opt-out signal handling logs, and vendor-contract amendment evidence. Activity language fails ("update privacy notice"); evidence language passes ("privacy notice v8.0 deployed across in-scope channels with sample-test of presentation rate").
