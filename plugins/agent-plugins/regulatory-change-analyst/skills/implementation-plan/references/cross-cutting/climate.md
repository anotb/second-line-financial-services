# Cross-cutting overlay — climate

Loaded when the engagement scope flags climate. Implementation plans triggered by or touching climate-risk rules carry distinct data, governance, and disclosure patterns.

## Large-bank climate-risk principles (Interagency)

For banks $100B+ in assets, the OCC, FRB, and FDIC's Principles for Climate-Related Financial Risk Management (October 2023) frame climate-risk management. Implementation plans integrate with the bank's enterprise risk management framework. Workstream elements typically include:

- governance (board and management oversight);
- policies, procedures, and limits;
- strategic planning;
- risk management (identification, measurement, monitoring of physical and transition risks);
- data, risk measurement, and reporting;
- scenario analysis;
- third-party / vendor inclusion of climate risk.

The principles are not a rulemaking and do not carry a fixed compliance date; the plan's effective date follows the bank's internal target and any supervisory letter expectations.

## SEC climate disclosure rule

The SEC's Climate-Related Disclosures rule (March 2024 final, with subsequent litigation pause) brings disclosure requirements for public companies. Implementation plans triggered by the rule (where it is operative) cover Regulation S-K Item 1500 series and Regulation S-X Article 14 disclosures, including governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics (Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions for accelerated and large accelerated filers, with phased dates).

The plan's status reflects the rule's litigation posture; where the rule is paused, the plan continues at a slower cadence as a contingency exercise. Voluntary frameworks (ISSB IFRS S2, TCFD, CDP) often run in parallel.

## State-level climate disclosure (California)

California SB 253 (Climate Corporate Data Accountability Act) and SB 261 (Climate-Related Financial Risk Act) impose climate-disclosure requirements on companies doing business in California above specified revenue thresholds. The plan's scope captures California operations and the entity-level revenue thresholds; California Air Resources Board (CARB) implementation regulations frame the technical requirements.

## NAIC climate-risk disclosure for insurers

For insurers, the NAIC Climate Risk Disclosure Survey (TCFD-aligned) is the framework adopted by lead-state DOIs. Implementation plans for an insurer's first or refreshed climate-risk disclosure integrate with the insurer's ORSA process (the climate-risk lens within ORSA) and the lead state's disclosure cycle.

## Workstream shape

Typical climate workstream pattern:

- governance updates (board oversight charter, named C-level role, committee inclusion);
- policy and procedure updates (climate risk in credit, market, operational, reputational risk frameworks);
- data sourcing for emissions, physical risk, and transition risk;
- scenario analysis build;
- third-party and vendor climate-risk inclusion in TPRM;
- disclosure narrative drafting where rules require disclosure;
- audit committee / board reporting cadence.

## Data-readiness as the binding constraint

Climate plans frequently encounter data-readiness as the binding constraint: emissions data quality, supply-chain Scope 3 visibility, climate-scenario data availability. The plan's risk register highlights data-readiness as a top risk, with explicit milestones for vendor data acquisition and internal data-quality remediation.

## Evidence patterns

Climate acceptance criteria emphasise documented governance (board minutes, committee charters, named oversight roles), documented scenario-analysis results with methodology, and where rules require disclosure, the actual disclosure document with iXBRL tagging or equivalent.
