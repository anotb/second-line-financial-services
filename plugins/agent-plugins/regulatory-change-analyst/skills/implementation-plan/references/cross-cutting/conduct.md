# Cross-cutting overlay — conduct

Loaded when the engagement scope flags conduct. Implementation plans triggered by or touching unfair-deceptive-or-abusive-acts-or-practices (UDAAP), fair-lending, fair-treatment, or consumer-redress findings carry distinct customer-impact and remediation patterns.

## UDAAP remediation under CFPB and FTC

Implementation plans triggered by a UDAAP finding (CFPB, FTC, OCC, FRB, FDIC, NCUA) cover three dimensions:

- the act or practice itself (cease the conduct, change the disclosure, change the product feature);
- the affected-population identification and consumer redress;
- the prospective controls that prevent recurrence.

For consent-order-driven UDAAP remediation, the order generally specifies the remediation framework, the redress methodology, the monitor or independent consultant role, and the reporting cadence. The implementation plan inherits the order's structure.

## Consumer-redress design

Redress milestones cover identifying the affected population (often the hardest analytical step), calculating the redress amount per consumer, designing the disbursement mechanism (account credit, check, electronic payment), drafting consumer communications, and reporting the disbursement to the regulator. The plan names the redress methodology and the reconciliation process.

For disbursement to consumers no longer at the firm, the plan addresses last-known-address handling, escheatment, and the regulator-acceptable retry process. For disbursement above tax-reporting thresholds, the plan addresses 1099 reporting.

## Fair-lending remediation

Implementation plans for fair-lending findings (ECOA, Reg B, FHA, HMDA, state fair-lending laws) carry pricing, underwriting, marketing, and steering dimensions. Remediation generally includes:

- statistical-analysis update on the affected loan population;
- consumer redress where pricing or denial harms are quantified;
- model and overlay review (where credit decisioning is implicated);
- training across in-scope underwriters and originators;
- ongoing monitoring with HMDA, ECOA, and state-specific testing programmes;
- second-line independent fair-lending review.

The CFPB ECOA examination procedures and the OCC, FRB, FDIC fair-lending examination procedures frame the supervisory expectations. For mortgage-side fair-lending, HMDA reporting under Regulation C is the data source.

## Sales-practices and conduct overlays

For sales-practices remediation (overdraft, deposit-account fee structures, add-on products, telesales, account-opening incentive plans), the plan covers:

- product-design changes;
- compensation and incentive review;
- sales-process control updates;
- complaint-handling integration;
- consumer redress;
- monitoring through complaint analytics, mystery shopping, and call-recording sampling.

## Complaint-handling

Implementation plans triggered by complaint-handling findings name the policy, the complaint-categorisation taxonomy, the SLAs, the root-cause-analysis process, and the trending and reporting cadence to senior management and the board. CFPB and OCC examiner expectations on complaint-handling frame the design; published consent orders inform the structural pattern.

## Workstream shape

Typical conduct workstream pattern:

- act-or-practice cessation (often immediate, ahead of the rest of the plan);
- affected-population identification (data-heavy);
- redress design and disbursement;
- consumer communications;
- prospective control build;
- training;
- monitoring programme update;
- regulator reporting cadence.

## Governance shape

Conduct-driven plans typically run with named board oversight given the customer-impact dimension. For consent-order remediation, monthly or quarterly board reporting is the practitioner pattern. The fair-lending committee, conduct committee, or consumer-protection committee is generally the senior-management body.

## Evidence patterns

Conduct acceptance criteria emphasise customer-facing evidence: redress disbursement reconciliation, consumer communication delivery confirmation, complaint-trending dashboards, mystery-shopping results, fair-lending statistical-analysis output. Counts of customers affected, dollars disbursed, and complaints received are the operative metrics.

## Plan posture

Conduct plans triggered by a public consent order generally carry `medium` confidence at most pre-closure; the monitor or independent consultant's confirmation is the gating step for closure attestation, not the firm's internal sign-off.
