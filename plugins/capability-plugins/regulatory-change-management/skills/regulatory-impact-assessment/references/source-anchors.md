# Source anchors — regulatory-impact-assessment

The named, dated regulatory sources this skill anchors to. Sector and cross-cutting overlays carry the rest. SKILL.md cites this file by path and does not restate excerpts.

## Federal banking — heightened-standards and supervisory frame

- **12 CFR Part 30 Appendix B — Interagency Guidelines Establishing Standards for Safety and Soundness**. The interagency safety-and-soundness operational-and-managerial standards (OCC, FRB, FDIC apply parallel guidelines under their respective implementing regulations). Anchors general operational standards (internal controls, information systems, audit, asset quality). https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-I/part-30 .

- **12 CFR Part 30 Appendix D — OCC Guidelines Establishing Heightened Standards** (effective September 11, 2014; amended 2024). The OCC's heightened-standards framework for insured national banks, federal savings associations, and federal branches with average total consolidated assets of $50B or more. §I introduction; §II definitions and scope; §III risk governance framework; §IV roles and responsibilities; §V policies, procedures, and standards; §VI independent risk management; §VII internal audit. The framework is the second-line authority hook for impact assessments at large national banks; the assessment's policy and control impact reads against the framework's risk-governance and independent-risk-management expectations. FDIC and FRB do not have a direct parallel to Appendix D; their supervisory framing for large institutions runs through SR letters and FDIC FILs (SR 16-11 large-financial-institution risk management; SR 95-51 risk-management ratings) rather than a unified appendix. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-I/part-30/appendix-Appendix%20D .

- **OCC Bulletin 2013-29 — Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management Guidance**. The OCC's prior third-party-risk guidance, supplanted by the June 2023 interagency guidance. https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2013/bulletin-2013-29.html .

- **Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management**, OCC FRB FDIC, June 2023 (release nr-ia-2023-53). The operative third-party-risk-management guidance for federally supervised banks; section II program elements; section III risk-based diligence; section IV ongoing monitoring; section V termination. https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2023/nr-ia-2023-53.html .

- **Joint Interagency Revised Guidance on Model Risk Management** (OCC Bulletin 2026-13 / FRB SR 26-2 / FDIC FIL-15-2026, April 17, 2026). One document issued through three agency channels with the same date, text, and signatories. Supersedes and replaces SR 11-7 / OCC Bulletin 2011-12 (April 4, 2011) and SR 21-8 (Interagency Statement on Model Risk Management for BSA/AML Compliance, April 9, 2021). Section II carries definitions, scope, and the threshold relevance for banking organizations over $30 billion in total assets; sections cover model identification and inventory, validation, ongoing monitoring, and governance. Caveats the assessment carries: the joint guidance is non-binding (does not set forth enforceable standards or prescriptive requirements; non-compliance does not result in supervisory criticism per Section I, footnote 1) and is "expected to be most relevant to banking organizations with over $30 billion in total assets"; generative AI and agentic AI models are explicitly out of scope (footnote 3 of the joint guidance text), with principles covering only traditional statistical / quantitative models and non-generative, non-agentic AI models — where the rule under assessment touches GenAI or agentic AI, AI scope routes to NIST AI RMF, NIST AI 600-1, ISO/IEC 42001, or the EU AI Act rather than to the bulletin; the bulletin contains zero references to NIST AI RMF or NIST AI 600-1. Operative links: https://occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-13a.pdf ; https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602.htm ; https://www.fdic.gov/news/financial-institution-letters/2026/agencies-revise-interagency-model-risk-management-guidance .

- **Superseded — SR 11-7 / OCC Bulletin 2011-12 (April 4, 2011) and SR 21-8 (April 9, 2021).** Retained as citation anchors only because pre-April-17-2026 examination evidence, validation reports, and prior firm policies frequently reference them by name. Cite as superseded when reading historical evidence. https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1107.htm ; https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2108.htm .

- **FRB SR 13-19 — Guidance on Managing Outsourcing Risk** (December 5, 2013). The FRB's vendor-risk-management guidance; supplanted in part by the June 2023 interagency guidance for federally supervised banks but still cited for examination convention. https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1319.htm .

- **FFIEC BSA/AML Examination Manual** (current edition) [verify edition date and section]. The interagency manual structure that drives BSA/AML impact-assessment scoping; the manual's CDD, beneficial-ownership, monitoring, and SAR sections are the chapter spine for AML-rule impact reads. https://bsaaml.ffiec.gov/manual .

- **FDIC FILs (Financial Institution Letters)** indexed by year [verify specific FIL number per assessment]. The FDIC's primary supervisory-communication channel; FILs carry FDIC-specific scoping detail for state non-member banks and FDIC-insured state savings associations. https://www.fdic.gov/news/financial-institution-letters/ .

## CFPB — circulars, bulletins, advisory opinions

- **CFPB Circulars** indexed at https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/circulars/ . Circulars were the CFPB's policy guidance vehicle for supervisory and enforcement positions. **A broad set of CFPB Circulars (UDAAP, overdraft / NSF, adverse-action with complex algorithms, account reopening, BNPL-related interpretive material) was withdrawn by the Bureau on May 12, 2025; treat as historical only.** Each circular still carries a circular number (e.g., Circular 2022-03 on adverse-action notices using algorithms — withdrawn May 12, 2025); the assessment cites the circular by its number with the withdrawal flag, and anchors the operative position to the underlying regulatory text.

- **CFPB Bulletins** indexed at https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/supervisory-guidance/ [verify section in specific bulletin]. Bulletins predate the circular format and remain operative for supervisory-guidance pre-circular.

- **CFPB Supervisory Highlights** (periodic) [verify edition and section]. The public summary of recent examination findings; the assessment's strategy lens reads against the most recent edition for examiner-posture signal. https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/supervisory-highlights/ .

- **12 CFR 1002 (Regulation B) §1002.9 — Adverse Action Notices**. The substantive adverse-action notice rule that anchors the AI/ML adverse-action circular. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1002 .

- **12 CFR 1002.107 — Small Business Lending (Section 1071)** [verify subsection labels]. The 1071 small-business-lending data-collection rule. **§1071 revised final rule, May 1, 2026: single 1,000-covered-transactions threshold; effective June 30, 2026; compliance January 1, 2028 — supersedes the 2023 tiered phase-in schedule.** https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/final-rules/small-business-lending-under-the-equal-credit-opportunity-act-regulation-b/ .

## SEC and FINRA — final rules, adopting releases, examination priorities

- **Federal Register**, the SEC adopting-release publication channel. Each SEC final rule carries a release number (e.g., Release No. 33-XXXXX) and a Federal Register citation [verify release number and FR citation per rule]; the assessment cites the release rather than a press summary. https://www.federalregister.gov/agencies/securities-and-exchange-commission .

- **SEC final rules and adopting releases** indexed at https://www.sec.gov/rules/final.shtml [verify release number and section per rule]. The adopting release contains the substantive analysis the impact assessment reads against, including the agency's own cost-and-benefit analysis (a regulator-specific overlay on the OMB Circular A-4 frame).

- **SEC cybersecurity disclosure rule** (17 CFR 229.106 and Form 8-K Item 1.05) [verify section labels in final adopting release]. https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/2023/33-11216.pdf .

- **SEC climate-related disclosure rule** (Release Nos. 33-11275; 34-99678, March 6 2024). Status: the SEC voluntarily stayed compliance pending judicial review in April 2024; not currently operative. State-level climate disclosure (California SB 253 / SB 261) and EU CSRD remain operative for in-scope filers regardless of SEC posture; the assessment cites whichever climate regime actually binds the firm, not the stayed federal rule. https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2024/33-11275.pdf .

- **SEC Marketing Rule (Investment Advisers Act Rule 206(4)-1)** [verify subsection labels in final adopting release]. https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/2020/ia-5653.pdf .

- **SEC Division of Examinations annual examination priorities** (most recent letter) [verify section in most recent letter]. The published annual statement of focus areas; the assessment's strategy lens reads against this for examiner-posture signal. https://www.sec.gov/exams .

- **FINRA Rule book** indexed at https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules . FINRA rule-filing alignment matters when an SEC adopting release implicates FINRA rule changes (the FINRA filing follows under SEC Rule 19b-4) [verify Rule 19b-4 subsection].

- **FINRA Annual Regulatory Oversight Report** (annual) [verify section in most recent annual report]. Combined examination priorities and findings; the assessment's strategy lens reads against this for FINRA-side examiner posture. https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/reports .

## NYDFS and state regulators — Industry Letters, regulations, market-conduct frame

- **NYDFS 23 NYCRR Part 500 — Cybersecurity Requirements for Financial Services Companies**. §500.2 program; §500.3 policy; §500.4 CISO; §500.5 testing; §500.7 access privileges; §500.11 third-party SP security policy; §500.17 notification. https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance/cybersecurity .

- **NYDFS Industry Letters** indexed at https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance . NYDFS publishes Industry Letters that shape examination posture for cybersecurity (Part 500), virtual currency (Part 200), insurance market conduct, AI use in insurance underwriting, and other topics [verify date and section in specific letter relevant to the assessment]. The October 2024 Industry Letter on cybersecurity risks arising from GenAI is a recurring impact-assessment trigger.

- **NAIC Model Laws and Regulations** (current edition) [verify model-law number and section per assessment]. State-by-state adoption variance is the operative effective-date driver for insurance assessments; a model law passed by the NAIC enters force only as states adopt it, and the adoption pace differs across states. https://content.naic.org/ .

- **State insurance department regulations** (state-by-state) [verify state-specific regulation citation]. State DOIs adopt NAIC model laws subject to local statutory variance; the assessment's geography determination reads against the state-by-state adoption status.

- **State money-transmitter licensing regulations** (state-by-state) [verify state-specific licensing-statute citation]. The assessment cites state MTM statutes when the rule implicates state-licensed money transmission. The Conference of State Bank Supervisors maintains a state-by-state index. https://www.csbs.org/ .

## OMB Circular A-4 and federal regulatory-analysis convention

- **OMB Circular A-4 — Regulatory Analysis** (originally September 17, 2003; revised November 9, 2023). Status: the November 2023 revision was rescinded in early 2025 and the original September 17, 2003 version was reinstated as the operative federal regulatory-analysis framework. The 2003 text is the operative posture; the 2023 revision sits as historical-only. Section structure (baseline construction, benefits and costs, distributional effects, alternatives) is comparable across both versions, but specific methodological and distributional-analysis instructions differ. The assessment's cost-to-comply read calibrates against agency cost-and-benefit analyses prepared under whichever A-4 was operative when the agency drafted the analysis. The 2003 version: https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/legacy_drupal_files/omb/circulars/A4/a-4.pdf .

- **Executive Order 12866 — Regulatory Planning and Review** (September 30, 1993; as amended) [verify section in current executive-order text]. The framework under which federal agencies submit significant rulemakings to OIRA review and publish regulatory-impact analyses; the assessment's strategy lens reads against the agency's RIA when one is published. https://www.archives.gov/files/federal-register/executive-orders/pdf/12866.pdf .

- **Federal Register impact-analysis convention**. Each Federal Register adopting release for a significant rule includes an "Effects of the Rule," "Regulatory Flexibility Act analysis," "Paperwork Reduction Act analysis," "Unfunded Mandates Reform Act analysis," and "Congressional Review Act analysis" section [verify section labels]. The assessment's source-identification block reads against these sections for the agency's own scoping read; the firm-side read calibrates against, and where appropriate diverges from, the agency analysis. https://www.federalregister.gov/ .

## ISO/IEC 42001 and NIST AI RMF — AI-related rule changes

- **ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — Artificial Intelligence Management System**. §6 planning (risk and opportunity, AI-impact-assessment); §7 support (resources, competence, awareness, communication, documented information); §8 operation (operational planning and control, AI-impact-assessment process); §9 performance evaluation. The assessment's AI-related impact reads against §6 risk-and-opportunity and §8 operational planning when the rule is AI-anchored. https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html .

- **NIST AI RMF 1.0** (January 2023). Govern function (GOVERN 1-6); Map function; Measure function; Manage function. The assessment's AI-rule impact reads against the Govern function's risk-management policy, accountability, and stakeholder-engagement subcategories. https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework .

## Industry associations — strategy-lens engagement channels

The skill names industry-association engagement as a strategy posture in the assessment. The named associations operate public-comment-letter channels and supervisory-engagement meetings. Public sources only. Trade-association engagement channels are not regulatory citations; the assessment cites the association's public-comment-letter or position paper by document title and date when it is the source of a posture claim.

Banking and credit union: Bank Policy Institute (BPI; large-bank engagement), American Bankers Association (ABA), Independent Community Bankers of America (ICBA), National Association of Federally-Insured Credit Unions (NAFCU), Credit Union National Association (CUNA), Mid-Size Bank Coalition of America (MBCA).

Capital markets: Securities Industry and Financial Markets Association (SIFMA), Investment Company Institute (ICI), Investment Adviser Association (IAA), Managed Funds Association (MFA), Alternative Investment Management Association (AIMA).

Insurance: American Council of Life Insurers (ACLI), American Property Casualty Insurance Association (APCIA), AHIP (health), National Association of Mutual Insurance Companies (NAMIC), National Association of Insurance and Financial Advisors (NAIFA).

Mortgage and consumer finance: Mortgage Bankers Association (MBA), Consumer Bankers Association (CBA), Financial Technology Association (FTA), American Fintech Council (AFC).

The strategy-lens section names where the firm aligns with which association on the rule under assessment, where the firm diverges from industry consensus, and what that divergence implies for the firm's engagement posture.
