# Cross-cutting overlay: conduct — regulatory-impact-assessment

Load this overlay when the rule under assessment touches UDAP / UDAAP, fair lending, fair claims, market conduct, suitability and care obligations, complaint handling, or junk-fee or anti-discrimination themes. Cross-cutting overlays do not change the assessment's spine; they tighten the source-anchor list and the named-policy and named-control patterns the assessment reads against.

## Named-policy and named-control patterns the rule may touch

When the rule is conduct-anchored, the impact assessment's policy-impact section typically references one or more:

- **UDAP / UDAAP Programme** (UDAAP risk assessment; complaint-channel intake and root-cause analysis; advertising and marketing review; product-development review; fee disclosure review).
- **Fair Lending Programme** (anti-discrimination policy; redlining-risk analysis; pricing-disparity-monitoring; HMDA data integrity; CRA programme; ECOA reason-code generation; second-look programme; fair-lending model risk review).
- **Fair Claims Programme** (insurance claims-handling fairness; SCRA / MLA service-member protections; FCRA compliance in adjudication; complaint-channel intake).
- **Marketing and Advertising Review Programme** (review-and-approval workflow; substantiation requirements; testimonial and endorsement requirements under Reg B / Reg Z / FINRA 2210 / SEC Marketing Rule).
- **Suitability and Care Programme** (Reg BI; FINRA 2111; IAA fiduciary duty; NAIC #275 annuity suitability).
- **Complaint Management Programme** (intake; categorisation; root-cause analysis; resolution timing; trend reporting; regulator-complaint coordination — CFPB consumer complaint database; state AG complaint coordination; FINRA Form U4 / U5 customer-complaint reporting).
- **Servicemember Programme** (SCRA / MLA programme; opt-in identification; rate-cap programme; foreclosure protection).
- **Servicing Programme** (mortgage servicing under Reg X / Reg Z; loss-mitigation; force-placed-insurance; escrow administration).
- **Debt Collection Programme** (Reg F implementation; communication restrictions; validation notices).
- **Credit Reporting Programme** (FCRA furnisher accuracy; dispute investigation; reinvestigation timing; e-OSCAR cycle).
- **Pricing-Fee Programme** (junk-fee remediation; fee transparency; fee-disclosure-rule compliance).

## Reporting-and-disclosure-impact patterns

Conduct rules routinely change the reporting-and-disclosure surface:

- **Public disclosures**: HMDA LAR (Reg C) annual public release; CRA Public File and Public Disclosure Statement; CFPB consumer complaint database firm responses; mortgage-servicing periodic statements (Reg Z and Reg X); fee-disclosure schedules (Reg DD and Reg E).
- **Regulator filings**: HMDA LAR; CRA performance evaluation reports; CFPB 1071 small-business-lending register; SCRA quarterly reports for high-volume servicers; FINRA Form U4 / U5 amendments for customer complaints; NAIC Market Conduct Annual Statement (MCAS).
- **Board reporting**: UDAAP risk assessment; fair-lending committee reporting; complaint-trend reporting; pricing-fee oversight reporting.
- **Examiner expectations**: CFPB examination scope on UDAAP, fair lending, mortgage origination and servicing, debt collection, credit reporting; OCC, FRB, FDIC fair-lending examination cycle; state-DOI market-conduct examination cycle (every 3-5 years typically); SEC Division of Examinations adviser-suitability and broker-dealer Reg BI examination cycle.

## Strategy-lens patterns specific to conduct rules

The conduct strategy lens has identifiable patterns:

- **Industry-association alignment**: ABA fair-lending committee; CBA (Consumer Bankers Association) UDAAP and fair-lending engagement; MBA (Mortgage Bankers Association) servicing and origination engagement; ACA International (debt collection) on Reg F; Consumer Reporting Industry Association on FCRA topics; NAFCU and CUNA on credit-union-specific conduct topics; SIFMA on Reg BI and adviser fiduciary; ICI on fund-side suitability and care; ACLI / APCIA / NAIFA on insurance conduct themes.
- **Examiner-posture-shift signal**: CFPB Director speeches and Supervisory Highlights drive UDAAP and fair-lending posture; OCC, FRB, FDIC fair-lending interagency communications; state AG enforcement actions on UDAAP and fair-lending themes (state UDAP statutes parallel federal UDAAP and frequently lead enforcement); state DOI market-conduct enforcement.
- **Comment-letter and engagement convention**: CFPB rulemakings on UDAAP-adjacent themes (junk fees, overdraft, NSF, mortgage-servicing) generate substantial industry engagement. Trade-association letters carry the consensus posture; firm-direct letters carry firm-specific divergences. Public consumer-advocacy comment letters set the counter-narrative the firm reads against.
- **Litigation posture**: CFPB rules in litigation (CFPB structure-related, specific final-rule challenges) affect effective-date posture and the firm's compliance pathway. The assessment names the litigation status and the firm's read of the litigation outcome's implication.
- **State-AG and multistate posture**: state attorneys general bring UDAP cases under state statutes (Massachusetts c. 93A; New York Executive Law §63(12); California UCL §17200) [verify section]. Multistate AG coalitions on specific topics drive cross-state enforcement themes. The firm's strategy lens includes state-AG engagement.
- **Fair-lending model risk specific posture**: rules touching credit-decisioning models (the CFPB Circular on adverse-action notices using AI/ML; Reg B reason-code rules; HUD's disparate-impact framework) carry a specific fair-lending-model-risk handoff that engages MRM and the fair-lending officer in tandem. The strategy lens names the handoff.
- **Junk-fee and fee-rule posture**: CFPB junk-fee themes have generated industry-wide engagement and litigation. The firm's strategy lens names the firm's junk-fee revenue exposure, the trade-association coordination on junk-fee comment letters, the litigation-posture reading on the Credit Card Penalty Fees Rule, and the firm's pricing-strategy implications.
- **Escalation triggers**: For UDAAP-implicating rules, escalation typically runs to the chief compliance officer, the head of consumer compliance, and the head of the affected business. For fair-lending implicating rules, the fair-lending officer and the head of the affected lending business are operative; for material-pricing-disparity findings the escalation runs to the CRO and general counsel. For market-conduct-implicating insurance rules, the chief compliance officer per line of business and the head of underwriting and head of claims are operative; multistate market-conduct findings escalate to general counsel.

## When the conduct overlay applies in part vs full

Load the overlay in full when the rule's primary subject is UDAP / UDAAP, fair lending, fair claims, market conduct, suitability and care, complaint handling, junk-fee, anti-discrimination, or servicemember protection. Load the overlay in part when the rule has a conduct dimension among other dimensions (e.g., a privacy rule with a fair-lending sub-component; a marketing-rule update with a UDAAP overlay). Do not load the overlay when the rule is privacy-anchored or cyber-anchored without a conduct dimension; load the privacy or cyber overlay instead.
