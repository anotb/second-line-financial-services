# Cross-cutting overlay: conduct

Loaded when the policy in scope is conduct-anchored — UDAAP, fair lending, marketing review, complaint handling, sales-practice supervision, suitability, Reg BI, conduct-risk framework. The overlay does not change the change-log spine; it loads the named-source set and the materiality lens.

## Source-citation patterns specific to conduct diffs

- CFPB UDAAP exam manual (Supervision and Examination Manual, UDAAP chapter) — primary reference for UDAAP-anchored policy diffs.
- ECOA (15 USC §1691) and Reg B (12 CFR Part 1002) — equal credit opportunity for credit-product policy diffs.
- HMDA (12 CFR Part 1003) — for mortgage-data-collection policy diffs.
- CFPB Section 1071 (12 CFR 1002.107) — small-business credit data collection.
- FINRA Rule 2111 (suitability) and SEC Reg BI — broker-dealer conduct.
- SEC Marketing Rule (17 CFR §275.206(4)-1) — adviser conduct in marketing.
- NAIC Market Conduct Examiners Handbook — for insurer conduct policy diffs.

## Materiality patterns specific to conduct diffs

- Customer-impact framing is the operative materiality lens. A change that affects how customers are treated, what disclosures they receive, or how their complaints are handled lands as material almost by default.
- Fair-lending-relevant changes (credit pricing, underwriting standards, servicing practices) carry both UDAAP and ECOA exposure and route to the fair-lending committee or named fair-lending officer where one exists.
- Marketing-review policy changes tighten or loosen the queue's review criteria; downstream impact almost always touches the marketing-review training and the recordkeeping configuration for marketing communications.
- Complaint-handling policy changes touch the CMS's consumer-complaint-response pillar (for CFPB-supervised firms) and the complaint-data-reporting workflow.

## Downstream-impact patterns specific to conduct diffs

- Customer-facing communication is more common in conduct diffs than in any other policy domain; disclosure-template revisions ripple to the customer-disclosure inventory.
- Frontline training and call-center scripting are common downstream artifacts; the audience-specific-function block frequently fires for conduct diffs.
- Sales-incentive and compensation policy changes interact with conduct policy changes; where both touch the same operative practice, cross-policy ripple flags reconciliation work.
- Material conduct policy changes commonly trigger fresh attestations from licensed personnel (registered representatives, mortgage loan originators, insurance producers) where the change touches their conduct expectations.
