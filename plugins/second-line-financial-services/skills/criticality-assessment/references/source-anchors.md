# Source anchors — criticality-assessment

The named, dated regulatory sources this skill anchors to. SKILL.md cites this file by path and does not restate excerpts. Sector-specific anchors live in the corresponding overlay files.

## Primary frame — third-party criticality

- **Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management** (OCC Bulletin 2023-17 / FRB SR 23-4 / FDIC FIL-29-2023, issued June 6, 2023). Risk-based application of third-party risk management; the guidance does not prescribe a fixed tier scheme. More rigorous oversight is expected for activities that pose higher risk; criticality is set proportionate to risk [verify section numbering across the released bulletin]. OCC release: https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2023/nr-ia-2023-53.html . FDIC release: https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2023/pr23047.html . FRB release: https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20230606a.htm .

- **OCC Bulletin 2013-29 — Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management Guidance** (October 30, 2013). Historical framing for "critical activities". The June 2023 interagency guidance is the current operative anchor; 2013-29 is no longer the operative reference for new diligence cycles. Where examiners reference it, treat as supplemental context, not as the operative frame. https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2013/bulletin-2013-29.html .

- **FFIEC Information Technology Examination Handbook — Outsourcing Technology Services booklet** [verify current edition and section structure]. Risk-assessment criteria for technology service providers, including criticality and inherent-risk reads. https://ithandbook.ffiec.gov/ .

## Recovery objectives and impact tolerance

- **FFIEC Information Technology Examination Handbook — Business Continuity Management booklet** [verify current edition and section structure]. Business-impact-analysis section on RTO and RPO definitions and the firm-tolerance vs vendor-commitment distinction. The BIA is the anchor for the recovery-objective section of the criticality assessment. https://ithandbook.ffiec.gov/ .

- **BCBS Principles for Operational Resilience** (March 2021). Cross-jurisdictional anchor for the concept of important business services and impact tolerance; substitutability and third-party arrangements considered as part of the IBS resilience read. https://www.bis.org/bcbs/publ/d516.htm .

- **UK PRA SS1/21 (Operational resilience: impact tolerances for important business services, March 2021; updated March 2022)** and **SS2/21 (Outsourcing and third-party risk management, March 2021)**. UK overlay only. Operational-resilience compliance milestone was 31 March 2025. PS7/26 with accompanying SS1/26 introduces a UK incident-reporting and material-third-party-arrangement-reporting framework and updates SS2/21 expectations; verify current SS2/21 version on the engagement date. The IBS framing and impact-tolerance concept anchor the UK call. https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/prudential-regulation/publication/2021/march/operational-resilience-impact-tolerances-for-important-business-services-ss . `[verify section]`

## DORA and EU criticality criteria

- **DORA — Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, Article 28**. General principles for sound management of ICT third-party risk. Structure: 28(1)-(2) management and strategy; 28(3) register of information at entity / sub-consolidated / consolidated levels distinguishing critical-or-important-function-supporting contracts with annual reporting to competent authorities; 28(4) prior-notification obligation for new arrangements supporting critical or important functions; 28(5) pre-contractual due diligence; 28(6) ongoing monitoring; 28(7) termination triggers; 28(8) documented and tested exit strategies. Applicable from 17 January 2025. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2554/oj .

- **DORA — Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, Article 6**. ICT risk management framework; the link from ICT risk to important functions. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2554/oj .

- **EBA Guidelines on outsourcing arrangements (EBA/GL/2019/02)**. Definition of "critical or important function" and the assessment criteria for criticality [verify section number]. https://www.eba.europa.eu/regulation-and-policy/internal-governance/guidelines-on-outsourcing-arrangements .

## Bank-specific anchors (banking overlay carries the detail)

- **12 CFR Part 30 Appendix D (OCC Heightened Standards)**. Risk-governance framework for $50B+ covered banks; the criticality call feeds the risk-governance framework and the board's third-party oversight [verify subsection labels]. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-I/part-30 .

- **Joint Statement on Banks' Arrangements with Third Parties to Deliver Bank Deposit Products and Services** (FDIC / OCC / FRB, July 25, 2024) [verify exact title and date]. Operative for sponsor-bank programs; deposit-program criticality call carries end-customer reconciliation, FBO subledger, deposit-insurance representation, and program-operator resilience considerations.

- **FFIEC inter-agency joint statement on community-bank fintech due diligence** (August 2021, FIL-59-2021) [verify exact title and date]. Community-bank-specific framework for fintech due diligence including the criticality lens. https://www.fdic.gov/news/financial-institution-letters/2021/fil21059.html .

## Insurance-specific anchors (insurance overlay carries the detail)

- **NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law (Model #668), §4.F** third-party service-provider obligations including risk-based criticality assessment.

- **NAIC Risk Management and Own Risk and Solvency Assessment Model Act (Model #505)** [verify model number]. Critical outsourced functions belong in the ORSA Summary Report; the criticality call feeds the ORSA narrative.

- **NAIC Insurance Holding Company System Regulatory Act (Model #440)** [verify model number] and **Holding Company Model Regulation (Model #450)** [verify model number]. Holding-company-act lens on intragroup arrangements.

- **NAIC Bulletin on the Use of Artificial Intelligence Systems by Insurers** (December 4, 2023). AIS Program third-party-AI accountability frames the AI-vendor criticality read at insurance carriers. https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/inline-files/2023-12-04%20Model%20Bulletin_Adopted_0.pdf [verify URL].

## Capital-markets-specific anchors (capital-markets overlay carries the detail)
