# Examples and troubleshooting

One section per worked example, then troubleshooting. Read only what you need.

## compensated-website-testimonial

### Example: compensated client testimonial on adviser website (April 2023 risk-alert pattern)

#### Scenario

An SEC-registered investment adviser publishes a video testimonial from an existing client on its public-facing website. The client receives a one-percent advisory-fee discount, in place for the next twelve months, in exchange for participating in the testimonial; the discount value over twelve months exceeds the prescribed compensation threshold under Rule 206(4)-1(b)(3). The testimonial runs above the fold on the firm's "Why work with us" landing page and is embedded in three other pages of the site. The CCO requests a marketing-rule-evidence pack on the website testimonial in response to a quarterly marketing-review cycle.

Source posture: public-plus-firm-policy-plus-evidence (the firm provides the video, the screen captures of the four landing pages on desktop and mobile, the written-promoter agreement, the ineligible-person screening attestation, the firm's testimonial oversight procedure under Rule 206(4)-7, and the firm's Reg S-P privacy notice).

#### Output sketch

The advertisement identification block flags the four pages on which the testimonial appears, the desktop and mobile screen captures, the dissemination status (in market for the past four months), and the language of the testimonial (English; the firm operates a Spanish-language site that does not currently carry the testimonial, which the pack flags as a separate item to verify).

Statement-type classification: testimonial under (a) two-prong definition (compensated communication offering advisory services) and under (c). The pack also flags the implicit endorsement of the firm by the published-author-credential the client mentions in the video; the (c) testimonial framework covers the use, but the pack notes the implicit-endorsement read for the conduct overlay.

Required-disclosures matrix runs the (c) clauses:

- Compensation status disclosure. Required clearly and prominently in the testimonial; finding: present in a footnote at the bottom of the page, not in the testimonial itself. The "clearly and prominently" standard reaches the location; a footnote at the bottom of a long landing page does not satisfy "clearly and prominently in the testimonial". Finding: inadequate.
- Client status disclosure. Required clearly and prominently in the testimonial; finding: present in the footnote, not in the testimonial. Finding: inadequate.
- Material conflicts disclosure. Required clearly and prominently in the testimonial; the testimonial does not address the conflict that the testimonial-giver receives a fee discount specifically tied to the testimonial. Finding: missing.

Testimonials and endorsements section:

- Compensated: yes; value over twelve months exceeds the prescribed threshold.
- Written agreement on file: yes; agreement signed at the time of recording, six months prior. Pack reads the agreement against (c)(3); finding: agreement covers the testimonial use but does not address the social-media reposts the firm has made of the testimonial; the pack flags the gap.
- Ineligible-person screen: attestation on file dated nine months prior to the recording date. The (c) requirement is for a screen at the agreement and on a continuing basis; the pack records the screen as stale and flags the re-screen requirement for the in-market period.
- Oversight evidence: the firm's testimonial oversight procedure under Rule 206(4)-7 is documented; the procedure was applied at recording but not since. Pack flags the gap.
- Recordkeeping pin: 204-2(a)(15); the agreement, the testimonial video, and the screening attestation are on file in the firm's testimonial repository; the social-media-repost log is partial.

Form ADV Item 5.L. cross-check: the firm reports use of testimonials on Form ADV; consistent.

Privacy overlay loads (the testimonial identifies the client by name and shows the client's face). The pack records:

- Client consent obtained: yes; the written agreement includes use-of-name and use-of-image consent for website use.
- Consent scope: the agreement does not address social-media reposts in the form the firm has made (the firm has reposted clips on its institutional LinkedIn and on its retail-targeted Instagram); the pack flags consent-scope as insufficient for the actual use.
- NPI disclosure review: the testimonial does not disclose account-level facts; status is no-npi-in-advertisement.
- Privacy-claim accuracy: the firm makes a "client confidentiality is at the core of how we operate" claim on the same page; the pack reads the claim against the testimonial use and against Reg S-P §248.6 / §248.7 notice content; finding: the claim and the testimonial use are reconcilable (the client consented), but the pack notes that the cumulative use across channels stretches the claim. The pack flags for counsel.

Findings list: (i) (c) testimonial-disclosure findings on compensation status, client status, and material conflicts (clearly-and-prominently failure on the first two; outright missing on the third); (ii) ineligible-person-screen staleness under (c); (iii) social-media-repost consent-scope and recordkeeping gap; (iv) (b)(2) substantiation finding on the implied capability claim that arises from the testimonial; (v) Reg S-P-adjacent finding on the cumulative-use stretch of the privacy claim. Severity: high on (i) and (iii); medium on (ii); medium on (iv); low on (v) pending counsel.

Recommended remediation: rework the testimonial-page layout to place the compensation, client-status, and conflicts disclosures clearly and prominently in or immediately adjacent to the testimonial itself (not in a page-bottom footnote); update the written agreement to cover the social-media reposts (or remove the reposts pending agreement amendment); run a fresh ineligible-person screen and document the continuing-basis re-screen cadence; reconcile the privacy claim with the cumulative use or moderate the claim. Hold-list: not full hold; the pack recommends the website testimonial remain in place pending the disclosure-layout fix (with a target date of 14 days), and recommends the social-media reposts be pulled pending the agreement amendment.

Reviewer determination: approved with edits; escalation to the CCO; counsel directs on the privacy-claim cumulative-use question.

#### Why this scenario matters

The April 17, 2023 SEC Risk Alert focus on testimonial / endorsement compliance [verify exact title] is the named EXAMS focus for this fact pattern. The example tests:

- The "clearly and prominently" standard reaches the location of the disclosure in the testimonial itself, not just its existence somewhere on the page.
- The written-agreement requirement covers the actual use; channel expansion (social-media reposts of a website testimonial) requires agreement-scope expansion.
- The ineligible-person screen is a continuing-basis requirement; staleness is a finding.
- The privacy overlay loads on testimonial use and the cumulative-channel use can stretch a privacy claim even where each individual use is consented.
- Form ADV Item 5.L. cross-check fires on testimonial use; the pack reads consistency between the firm's actual marketing inventory and its Form ADV response.

## hypothetical-performance-pitch-deck

### Example: hypothetical-performance pitch deck (September 2023 enforcement-sweep pattern)

#### Scenario

An SEC-registered investment adviser circulates a pitch deck to prospective institutional clients. The deck includes a "model allocation strategy" section showing seven years of backtested returns on a hypothetical portfolio constructed from the firm's research views, paired against a published benchmark. The deck reaches roughly forty institutional and family-office prospects through individual relationship-manager meetings and through a presentation at a sell-side investment-strategy conference where attendees received printed copies. The CCO requests a marketing-rule-evidence pack on the deck before the next dissemination wave.

Source posture: public-plus-firm-policy-plus-evidence (the firm provides the deck, the calculation worksheets, the audience-appropriateness policy text, and the distribution log).

#### Output sketch

The pack opens with the advertisement identification (deck v3, dated, conference-distribution and meeting-distribution channels, mixed institutional / family-office audience). Statement-type classification flags advertisement-general, performance, and hypothetical-performance (the model section). The pack records that the conference distribution is not one-on-one; the relationship-manager meetings are one-on-one but the (d)(6) hypothetical-performance conditions apply regardless of recipient count, so the one-on-one carve-out claim does not eliminate the (d)(6) review.

The required-disclosures matrix flags the hypothetical-performance disclosures as in scope. The substantiation file logs each material claim and the source. The performance-presentation review reads the gross-and-net pairing on the model series (gross only is presented; the firm has no published net series for the hypothetical because the strategy was never run live; the pack records the gap and the reviewer note that the rule's net-of-fees requirement applies to hypothetical performance).

The hypothetical-performance pre-conditions section is the load-bearing block:

- Audience-appropriateness policy. The firm has a written marketing-review policy under Rule 206(4)-7, but the policy does not specify the audience for which hypothetical performance is appropriate; it describes a generic review process. The pack records the policy gap.
- Procedure applied. The procedure is documented as having been "reviewed by marketing compliance"; the procedure does not show a specific determination that the hypothetical is appropriate for the institutional and family-office audience receiving this deck. The pack records the procedure gap.
- Criteria-and-assumptions disclosure. The deck contains a methodology footnote describing the universe and the rebalancing rules; the pack reads the footnote against the (d)(6)(ii) condition and finds it sufficient on universe but incomplete on rebalancing assumptions and on the survivorship-bias treatment.
- Risks-and-limitations disclosure. The deck contains a generic "past performance does not guarantee future results" disclaimer; the pack reads it against the (d)(6)(iii) condition and finds the disclaimer does not address the specific risks of the backtest (look-ahead bias risk; the absence of any actual trading; the absence of any actual investor experience). The pack flags the risks-and-limitations disclosure as incomplete.

The books-and-records evidence index pins the calculation worksheet retention under Rule 204-2(a)(11) and (a)(16); the worksheet exists for the model series but the underlying-account trail is partial (the worksheet references model positions, not actual accounts, which is correct for a hypothetical, but the source of the model positions and the input data are not pinned). The pack records the recordkeeping note.

Findings list: (i) performance-presentation finding under 206(4)-1(e) for the gross-only presentation of the hypothetical; (ii) hypothetical-performance pre-conditions findings under 206(4)-1(d)(6)(i), (ii), and (iii); (iii) recordkeeping finding under 204-2(a)(11) and (a)(16) for the partial input-data trail; (iv) (b)(2) finding for the unsubstantiated "model demonstrates the strategy" implication where the procedure to demonstrate audience-appropriateness was not applied. Severity: high on (ii) and (iv), medium on (i) and (iii).

The Form ADV Item 5.L. cross-check confirms the firm reports use of hypothetical performance on Form ADV; the consistency read is consistent on the use, but the firm's audience-appropriateness procedure does not match what the Form ADV response implies (a developed procedure).

Recommended remediation: amend the audience-appropriateness policy under Rule 206(4)-7 to specify the audience for which hypothetical performance is appropriate; document the specific procedure applied to this deck (and to future decks); add the missing rebalancing-assumption and survivorship-bias treatment to the methodology footnote; expand the risks-and-limitations disclosure to address the backtest-specific risks; pin the input-data source in the worksheet; either compute and present a net series (using a documented assumed-fee approach) or stop using the hypothetical until the net-of-fees presentation is in place. Hold-list: yes; the pack recommends the deck be pulled from the dissemination wave until the audience-appropriateness procedure is documented and the (d)(6) disclosures are updated.

The pack's reviewer determination: not approved; escalation to the CCO and to the head of distribution; counsel directs given the September 2023 enforcement sweep pattern.

#### Why this scenario matters

The September 2023 SEC enforcement sweep against nine investment advisers for alleged hypothetical-performance violations [verify exact release count and dates] is the named empirical reference for what the Commission treats as actionable on this fact pattern. The example tests:

- The (d)(6) conditions apply regardless of whether the communication is to one client or many; the one-on-one carve-out from the advertisement definition does not extend to hypothetical performance.
- The audience-appropriateness condition is a procedural requirement: the firm must have a policy and the policy must have been applied; a generic marketing-review policy does not satisfy the condition.
- The criteria-and-assumptions disclosure and the risks-and-limitations disclosure are both required and both have specific content; a generic past-performance disclaimer does not satisfy (d)(6)(iii).
- The gross-and-net pairing applies to hypothetical performance; the absence of any live track record does not eliminate the net-of-fees requirement.
- Form ADV Item 5.L. cross-check is part of the pack and inconsistencies are themselves a finding.

## troubleshooting

### Troubleshooting: marketing-rule-evidence

Recurring failure modes the drafter should preempt and the reviewer should catch.

#### 1. Misclassifying the artifact's statement type

The Marketing Rule's two-prong "advertisement" definition has carve-outs (one-on-one communications excluded except for hypothetical performance; extemporaneous oral communications excluded; certain regulatory communications excluded). Misclassification at the top of the pack produces the wrong evidence trail end-to-end. Read each component of the artifact independently (a deck routinely contains a performance presentation, a hypothetical-performance section, a third-party-rating logo, and a compensated testimonial in the same file); classify each component against the rule's definitions and apply the relevant disclosure mechanics per component.

#### 2. Treating one-on-one as a free ride for hypothetical performance

The (d)(6) hypothetical-performance pre-conditions apply regardless of whether the communication is to one client or many. The one-on-one carve-out from the advertisement definition does not eliminate (d)(6); the firm's audience-appropriateness policy and procedure must still be applied. This is the recurring September 2023 enforcement-sweep finding pattern.

#### 3. Treating "the disclosure mechanics tick the boxes" as the end of the analysis

Rule 206(4)-1(b) general prohibitions are the antifraud backbone. A representation that is technically compliant with the disclosure mechanics can still violate (b)(1), (b)(2), (b)(3), (b)(5), or (b)(6). The fair-and-balanced overlay (cross-cutting.md#conduct) reads cherry-picked performance, materially-misleading-by-implication framings, implicit endorsements via logos and association marks, and unbalanced risk disclosures.

#### 4. Confusing predecessor, related, model, and hypothetical performance

Each has separate conditions. A presentation often combines two or three categories. Map each performance series to the right category before running disclosures. A "model" series presented as the firm's track record is hypothetical; a series carried from a prior firm or prior personnel is predecessor (subject to portability conditions); a series of accounts within a strategy presented as a representative sample is extracted (with specific disclosure mechanics); a composite of all accounts in a strategy is related performance (with composite-construction discipline).

#### 5. Net-of-fees presentation errors

Net must be presented with the same prominence as gross, computed using the same methodology and time periods. The "one-on-one to a private fund investor" exception is narrow: actual one-on-one delivery; the firm provides on-request the information necessary to compute net-of-fees. Hypothetical performance is not exempt from the gross-and-net pairing requirement; the absence of any live track record does not eliminate the net-of-fees requirement (the firm computes a net series using a documented assumed-fee approach or it stops using the hypothetical).

#### 6. Stale ineligible-person screening

The (c) screen must be run at the agreement and on a continuing basis. A screening attestation older than the look-back window is a finding. The continuing-basis cadence is the firm's choice (annual is common, but the firm's policy under Rule 206(4)-7 sets the cadence and the procedure must show the cadence is met).

#### 7. Missing the "clearly and prominently in the testimonial itself" standard

The (c) testimonial / endorsement disclosure clauses (compensation, client status, material conflicts) must appear clearly and prominently. The standard reaches the location: a footnote at the bottom of a long landing page does not satisfy "clearly and prominently in the testimonial". Where the testimonial is a video, the disclosures must appear in the video at a point where the viewer is reasonably expected to see them; a closing card at minute 5:30 of a 5:32 video is not clear and prominent.

#### 8. Treating logos and association marks as outside the third-party-rating framework

A rating provider's logo or badge displayed in advertising is third-party-rating use. The (d) reasonable-basis read, the rating-period and methodology disclosures, and the compensation disclosures all apply. "It is just a logo" is not a defence.

#### 9. Missing the books-and-records pin for oral advertisements

Webinars, podcasts, conference talks, and recorded interviews are advertisements where the two-prong definition is met (and the extemporaneous-oral carve-out is not). Rule 204-2(a)(11) requires records sufficient to demonstrate compliance: the recording, the outline or transcript where used, the substantiation file for any material claim made orally. The retention obligation is the same as for written advertisements.

#### 10. Treating the calculation worksheet as a back-office artifact

Rule 204-2(a)(11) and (a)(16) require the worksheet, the methodology pin, the underlying-account trail, and the date-stamped attestation to be sufficient to demonstrate the calculation of any performance shown. A worksheet that cannot be produced renders the performance claim unsubstantiated for recordkeeping purposes regardless of whether the underlying calculation is correct. The pack closes the loop between the advertisement review and the performance-supporting records.

#### 11. Confusing the Marketing Rule with FINRA Rule 2210 and with Rule 482 / Rule 156

Same conduct, three different rule sets depending on entity and product. The Marketing Rule (Rule 206(4)-1) is the IA lane; FINRA Rule 2210 is the broker-dealer lane (institutional / retail / correspondence categorisation; pre-use approval; principal sign-off; recordkeeping; content standards); Rule 482 / Rule 156 is the registered-fund advertising lane. For dual registrants, both Marketing Rule and Rule 2210 may apply to the same communication. The pack scopes to the Marketing Rule lane; the BD-side review and the fund-side review route to the relevant compliance functions.

#### 12. Missing the Form ADV Item 5.L. cross-check

Inconsistencies between the firm's actual marketing inventory and its Form ADV Part 1A Item 5.L. responses are an EXAMS finding pattern. The pack runs the cross-check and flags any inconsistency in the residual-issues block.

#### 13. Treating the Marketing Rule as the consumer-financial UDAAP lane

The Marketing Rule lives in the Advisers Act §206 antifraud lane and the §206(4) rulemaking authority. Consumer-financial marketing under Dodd-Frank §1031 UDAAP is a different doctrinal lane reviewed under `marketing-claim-review`. Same word "marketing" in both, different statutory framework, different burden of proof, different examiner. Running the wrong skill produces the wrong analysis.

#### 14. Treating private-fund advisers as outside the rule

Private-fund advisers operate under the Marketing Rule on the same basis as other SEC-registered advisers. The rule does not distinguish public-fund vs. private-fund adviser status for general application; the only private-fund-specific provision is the narrow one-on-one private-fund-investor exception on net-of-fees presentation, which has its own conditions.

#### 15. Confusing the adviser-as-advertiser with the fund-as-advertiser

Where the advertisement promotes the adviser's advisory services to the fund (and to other prospective clients), the Marketing Rule governs. Where the advertisement promotes the fund itself as a security, the routing is Rule 482 / Rule 156. Many marketing pieces are dual-purpose; the pack flags the routing.
