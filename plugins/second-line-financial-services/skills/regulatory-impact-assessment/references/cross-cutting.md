## climate

### Cross-cutting overlay: climate — regulatory-impact-assessment

Load this overlay when the rule under assessment touches climate-related financial risk, climate disclosure, the federal banking agencies' climate-related principles for large banks, NAIC climate risk frameworks for insurers, SEC climate-disclosure rules (where in force), or international frameworks (TCFD, ISSB, EU CSRD) that apply to the firm's reporting perimeter. Cross-cutting overlays do not change the assessment's spine; they tighten the source-anchor list and the named-policy and named-control patterns the assessment reads against.

#### Named-policy and named-control patterns the rule may touch

When the rule is climate-anchored, the impact assessment's policy-impact section typically references one or more:

- **Climate Risk Governance and Strategy Programme** (board oversight of climate risk; senior-management responsibility; integration of climate risk into strategy and capital-and-liquidity planning).
- **Climate Risk Management Programme** (identification, measurement, monitoring, and control of physical and transition climate risks across the risk taxonomy: credit, market, liquidity, operational, legal, compliance, strategic, reputational).
- **Scenario-Analysis Programme** (climate scenario analysis with defined scenarios, time horizons, and use cases; the FRB Pilot Climate Scenario Analysis exercise patterns).
- **Climate-related Data Programme** (asset-level GHG-emissions data; physical-risk-asset-location data; transition-risk-counterparty data).
- **Climate Disclosure Programme** (Reg S-K Items 1500-1508 disclosures where in force; Reg S-X Article 14 financial-statement-level disclosures; NAIC Climate Risk Disclosure Survey response; CDP / TCFD / ISSB voluntary disclosure where elected).
- **Climate Risk Reporting Programme** (board reporting on climate risk; supervisory reporting where applicable; investor reporting).
- **Climate Risk Third-Party Programme** (third-party assessment of climate-related disclosure-data providers, scenario-analysis platforms, GHG-accounting platforms).
- **Insurance Underwriting Climate Programme** (where applicable, climate-related underwriting risk consideration; rate-and-form-filing climate justification under state insurance codes).

#### Reporting-and-disclosure-impact patterns

Climate rules routinely change the reporting-and-disclosure surface:

- **Public disclosures**: SEC Form 10-K Reg S-K Item 1500 disclosures (where in force); Reg S-X Article 14 financial-statement-level disclosures; voluntary TCFD or ISSB-aligned reports; CDP submissions.
- **Regulator filings**: NAIC Climate Risk Disclosure Survey response (state-by-state filings); California SB 253 GHG-emissions reporting and SB 261 climate-related-financial-risk reporting (where applicable); FIO data calls; EU CSRD reporting (where applicable).
- **Board reporting**: federal banking principles call for board oversight of climate risk and senior-management accountability; the firm's climate-related-financial-risk MIS feeds the board.
- **Examiner expectations**: federal banking agencies' principles drive examination-priority emphasis for $100B+ banks; NYDFS climate-risk-management framework drives state-supervisor examination posture for licensed insurers.

#### Strategy-lens patterns specific to climate rules

The climate strategy lens has identifiable patterns:

- **Industry-association alignment**: BPI climate working group; ABA climate working group; ACLI climate working group; SIFMA climate engagement; ICI fund-side climate engagement; the Risk Management Association climate-risk subcommittee; the Network for Greening the Financial System (NGFS) for central-bank-and-supervisor cross-jurisdictional engagement.
- **Litigation posture**: SEC climate-disclosure rule litigation has affected effective-date posture and the firm's compliance timeline. The assessment names the litigation status and the firm's read of the litigation outcome's implication for the compliance pathway.
- **Cross-jurisdictional coordination**: where the firm reports under SEC climate-disclosure (where in force), CSRD (EU), ISSB (where mandated by jurisdictions), California SB 253 / SB 261, and NAIC Climate Risk Disclosure Survey, the firm's reporting consolidation reduces duplicative-data burden. The impact assessment names the consolidation strategy.
- **Examiner-posture-shift signal**: federal banking agencies' interagency principles; FRB Pilot Climate Scenario Analysis communications; NYDFS Industry Letters; California DOI climate-related circulars; international-supervisor communications (ECB, BoE, MAS, HKMA, JFSA).
- **Stakeholder posture**: investor pressure (asset-manager net-zero commitments; proxy-advisory climate-related vote pattern); customer pressure (corporate-customer net-zero commitments flowing into supplier expectations); employee and community-stakeholder posture.
- **Escalation triggers**: For federally supervised banks at $100B+ in assets, the federal banking principles drive board-level engagement on climate-risk-management programme. For SEC registrants, the climate-disclosure rule (where in force) drives audit-committee and disclosure-committee engagement. For state-regulated insurers in mandatory-disclosure states, the climate-survey response is a chief-risk-officer or general-counsel-led artifact.

#### When the climate overlay applies in part vs full

Load the overlay in full when the rule's primary subject is climate-related financial risk, climate disclosure, climate-related-financial-risk management framework, or climate-related insurance underwriting. Load the overlay in part when the rule has a climate dimension among other dimensions (e.g., a broader sustainability rule with a climate-component sub-section). Do not load the overlay when the rule is sustainability-related but climate-tangential (the climate overlay is climate-specific; broader sustainability rules sit outside the spine of this overlay).

#### Reporting-and-disclosure-impact patterns

Conduct rules routinely change the reporting-and-disclosure surface:

- **Regulator filings**: HMDA LAR; CRA performance evaluation reports; CFPB 1071 small-business-lending register; SCRA quarterly reports for high-volume servicers; FINRA Form U4 / U5 amendments for customer complaints; NAIC Market Conduct Annual Statement (MCAS).
- **Board reporting**: UDAAP risk assessment; fair-lending committee reporting; complaint-trend reporting; pricing-fee oversight reporting.
- **Examiner expectations**: CFPB examination scope on UDAAP, fair lending, mortgage origination and servicing, debt collection, credit reporting; OCC, FRB, FDIC fair-lending examination cycle; state-DOI market-conduct examination cycle (every 3-5 years typically); SEC Division of Examinations adviser-suitability and broker-dealer Reg BI examination cycle.

#### Strategy-lens patterns specific to conduct rules

The conduct strategy lens has identifiable patterns:

- **Industry-association alignment**: ABA fair-lending committee; CBA (Consumer Bankers Association) UDAAP and fair-lending engagement; MBA (Mortgage Bankers Association) servicing and origination engagement; ACA International (debt collection) on Reg F; Consumer Reporting Industry Association on FCRA topics; NAFCU and CUNA on credit-union-specific conduct topics; SIFMA on Reg BI and adviser fiduciary; ICI on fund-side suitability and care; ACLI / APCIA / NAIFA on insurance conduct themes.
- **Examiner-posture-shift signal**: CFPB Director speeches and Supervisory Highlights drive UDAAP and fair-lending posture; OCC, FRB, FDIC fair-lending interagency communications; state AG enforcement actions on UDAAP and fair-lending themes (state UDAP statutes parallel federal UDAAP and frequently lead enforcement); state DOI market-conduct enforcement.
- **Comment-letter and engagement convention**: CFPB rulemakings on UDAAP-adjacent themes (junk fees, overdraft, NSF, mortgage-servicing) generate substantial industry engagement. Trade-association letters carry the consensus posture; firm-direct letters carry firm-specific divergences. Public consumer-advocacy comment letters set the counter-narrative the firm reads against.
- **Litigation posture**: CFPB rules in litigation (CFPB structure-related, specific final-rule challenges) affect effective-date posture and the firm's compliance pathway. The assessment names the litigation status and the firm's read of the litigation outcome's implication.
- **State-AG and multistate posture**: state attorneys general bring UDAP cases under state statutes (Massachusetts c. 93A; New York Executive Law §63(12); California UCL §17200) [verify section]. Multistate AG coalitions on specific topics drive cross-state enforcement themes. The firm's strategy lens includes state-AG engagement.
- **Fair-lending model risk specific posture**: rules touching credit-decisioning models (the CFPB Circular on adverse-action notices using AI/ML; Reg B reason-code rules; HUD's disparate-impact framework) carry a specific fair-lending-model-risk handoff that engages MRM and the fair-lending officer in tandem. The strategy lens names the handoff.
- **Junk-fee and fee-rule posture**: CFPB junk-fee themes have generated industry-wide engagement and litigation. The firm's strategy lens names the firm's junk-fee revenue exposure, the trade-association coordination on junk-fee comment letters, the litigation-posture reading on the Credit Card Penalty Fees Rule, and the firm's pricing-strategy implications.
- **Escalation triggers**: For UDAAP-implicating rules, escalation typically runs to the chief compliance officer, the head of consumer compliance, and the head of the affected business. For fair-lending implicating rules, the fair-lending officer and the head of the affected lending business are operative; for material-pricing-disparity findings the escalation runs to the CRO and general counsel. For market-conduct-implicating insurance rules, the chief compliance officer per line of business and the head of underwriting and head of claims are operative; multistate market-conduct findings escalate to general counsel.

#### When the conduct overlay applies in part vs full

Load the overlay in full when the rule's primary subject is UDAP / UDAAP, fair lending, fair claims, market conduct, suitability and care, complaint handling, junk-fee, anti-discrimination, or servicemember protection. Load the overlay in part when the rule has a conduct dimension among other dimensions (e.g., a privacy rule with a fair-lending sub-component; a marketing-rule update with a UDAAP overlay). Do not load the overlay when the rule is privacy-anchored or cyber-anchored without a conduct dimension; load the privacy or cyber overlay instead.

#### When the cyber overlay applies in part vs full

Load the overlay in full when the rule's primary subject is cyber, information security, computer-security incident notification, or AI-system cyber risk. #### Reporting-and-disclosure-impact patterns

Privacy rules routinely change the reporting-and-disclosure surface:

- **Public disclosures**: privacy notice (GLBA Privacy Rule annual delivery); CCPA notice at collection; CCPA notice at sale or sharing; opt-out-of-sale or opt-out-of-targeted-advertising disclosures; HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices.
- **Regulator notifications**: state breach-notification under each state's breach-notification law (timing and content vary by state); GLBA Safeguards Rule notification (FTC); SEC Reg S-P notification (2024 amendment) [verify final-rule timing]; HHS-CMS HIPAA breach notification (60 days, with state-by-state additional requirements); CFPB consumer-notification posture for material privacy events (typically driven by enforcement convention rather than rule).
- **Subject-rights reporting**: CCPA requires firms over a transaction-volume threshold to publish metrics on subject-rights-request volume and response timing in their privacy notice [verify CCPA threshold and metric content].
- **Board reporting**: GLBA Safeguards Rule (16 CFR Part 314) [verify section] requires written reporting to the board or governing body at least annually; state insurance data security model law parallel.

#### Strategy-lens patterns specific to privacy rules

The privacy strategy lens has identifiable patterns:

- **Industry-association alignment**: IAPP for cross-industry privacy engagement; the State Privacy and Security Coalition (SPSC) for state-comprehensive-privacy-law engagement; trade-association privacy committees (BPI, ABA, SIFMA, ICI, IAA, ACLI, FTA, AFC) for sector-specific engagement.
- **State-by-state engagement**: state attorney general offices (CCPA / CPRA enforcement is by California Attorney General and the California Privacy Protection Agency); Connecticut, Colorado, Virginia, Utah AGs; the multistate AG coalitions on specific topics.
- **Federal engagement**: CFPB on Section 1033 and on consumer-financial-data rules; FTC on GLBA Safeguards updates and on broader privacy themes; SEC on Reg S-P updates and on adviser-and-fund-side privacy; HHS-CMS on HIPAA topics; OFAC where sanctions data-handling intersects.
- **Cross-border posture**: where the firm operates internationally, EU DPAs and UK ICO are parallel-engagement points. The DPF, the EU adequacy decision, and SCCs are operative cross-border mechanisms.
- **Examiner-posture-shift signal**: CFPB Director speeches on consumer-financial-data; FTC enforcement pattern on GLBA Safeguards (recent enforcement actions against motor-vehicle dealers, mortgage companies, and others have signalled emphasis); state-AG enforcement under CCPA/CPRA; HHS-CMS breach-portal trend pattern.
- **Cross-rule coordination**: privacy rules routinely overlap (a CCPA/CPRA rule update plus a GLBA Privacy Rule update plus a Section 1033 rule update). The impact assessment names the cross-rule coordination needs (e.g., a single privacy notice that satisfies multiple regimes; a single data-inventory that supports multiple subject-rights regimes).

#### When the privacy overlay applies in part vs full

Load the overlay in full when the rule's primary subject is consumer-financial-data privacy, GLBA Privacy or Safeguards Rule, state privacy law, HIPAA, sectoral privacy (children, health, biometric), or cross-border data-transfer mechanisms. Load the overlay in part (only the relevant sections above) when the rule has a privacy dimension among other dimensions (e.g., a marketing-rule update with a consumer-data-handling sub-section). Do not load the overlay when the rule is GLBA Safeguards Rule technical-controls primary (load the cyber overlay instead, which carries the technical-controls anchor).
