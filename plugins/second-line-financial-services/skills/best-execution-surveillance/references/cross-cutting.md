## conduct

### Cross-cutting overlay — conduct (fiduciary duty, best-interest, conflict-as-input)

This overlay applies to best-execution surveillance because the duty itself is a conduct duty. Best execution is not merely a technical process; it is the periodic execution of a fiduciary or quasi-fiduciary obligation owed to clients. The conduct overlay loads automatically for this skill on any engagement where the institution owes a fiduciary, best-interest, or principal-supervisory duty connected to its trading and routing decisions. That covers every IA, every dual registrant on the IA side, every fund adviser, and every BD on the §5310 limb.

#### Where the conduct lens bites in this artifact

Three places. Read each as a separate review point even where the firm runs them together operationally.

##### 1. Conflict-as-input, not conflict-as-disclosure

The most-frequent finding in this lane is the artifact treating a conflict (PFOF, principal trade, agency cross, affiliated brokerage, soft dollar, commission-sharing arrangement) as a disclosure item instead of as an input to the best-execution determination itself. The conduct lens insists on the second framing.

For each conflict in scope, the surveillance must show:

- That the conflict was identified before the trading decision.
- That the conflict was weighed in the determination of how to route or execute, with a record of how the weighing went.
- That the residual conflict (after mitigation, where mitigation exists) sits within the firm's appetite and that the appetite was set by a function with the standing to set it (committee, board, CCO).
- That the disclosure to the client is consistent with how the firm actually treated the conflict (an aggressive disclosure paired with a routine routing pattern that ignores the disclosed conflict is itself a finding).

The conduct overlay is the place where the artifact tests whether the conflict treatment is real. The §206 fiduciary duty requires the adviser to act in the client's best interest, not just to disclose. The Robare Group line of cases (SEC v. Robare Group, Ltd., D.C. Cir. 2018, and the underlying SEC enforcement action) is the doctrinal anchor for the proposition that disclosure does not cure conflict where the underlying conduct does not match the disclosure. ##### 2. Customer-facing posture and the Form CRS / Reg BI line

Where the firm is a dual registrant or a BD subject to Regulation Best Interest, the customer-facing posture has its own conduct expectation. Reg BI's Care Obligation requires the broker-dealer recommending a securities transaction or strategy to a retail customer to exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill to understand the security or strategy and to have a reasonable basis to believe the recommendation is in the retail customer's best interest. Best-execution failures bleed into the Care Obligation analysis where the recommendation paired the customer with a routing path that could not deliver the price the recommendation implied.

The artifact's conduct block records: whether any sampled exception involved a recommended transaction (vs an unsolicited order); whether the recommendation's economic premise depended on execution quality the firm did not deliver; whether the customer disclosure (Form CRS for retail customers; Reg BI relationship summary) accurately characterised the routing arrangement.

##### 3. Best-execution committee culture and challenge

The conduct lens reads the committee minutes for substantive challenge, dissents, and follow-through, not just attendance and approvals. The pattern of every committee meeting closing with no dissents, no questions, and no follow-ups across a multi-quarter run is itself a culture finding. Examiners read minutes for evidence that the committee functioned; the absence of evidence is a finding.

The artifact's committee_pack block records dissents recorded, follow-ups assigned with owner and due date, follow-ups closed with evidence, and follow-ups still open past target date. The block is structured to make the absence of any of these visible.

#### Specific conduct themes by registration class

##### Investment adviser

- **Brokerage allocation across clients with different fee structures.** Where the adviser manages both wrap-fee and non-wrap accounts, or both fund and separate-account assets, the allocation methodology must not advantage one structure over another in routing or in execution-quality outcomes. The surveillance reads the per-trade allocation and the aggregate execution-quality outcomes per program for systematic divergence.
- **Conflicted soft-dollar use.** Mixed-use allocation that systematically defaults to research-eligible (and therefore client-paid via commissions) for products with material non-research utility is a conduct finding regardless of what the documentation says. The surveillance reads the allocation methodology against the actual usage pattern.
- **Directed brokerage at the client's instruction.** Where the client directs the adviser to use a specific broker, the adviser's best-execution duty is constrained but not eliminated. The artifact records the directed-brokerage population, whether the directed broker delivers reasonable execution quality, and whether the adviser has documented the constraint and discussed it with the client.

##### Broker-dealer

- **Retail-flow PFOF and the routing-quality conflict.** Where the BD routes retail flow to wholesalers paying for order flow, the surveillance must show that the routing decision turned on execution quality the wholesaler delivers, not on PFOF received. Comparing PFOF-receiving venues against non-PFOF venues on identical order types and sizes is the standard surveillance method.
- **Internalisation and crossing.** Where the BD internalises a portion of customer flow against its own market-maker desk, the surveillance reads the price-improvement metrics for internalised flow against away-routed flow. Systematic divergence in favour of the market-maker is a conduct finding.
- **Order-handling rule compliance.** Limit-order display rules (Rule 604), order-protection rules (Rule 611), and locked / crossed market handling sit alongside best execution as related conduct duties. The artifact carries findings here as related items where the surveillance surfaces them.

##### Fund adviser

- **Affiliated-broker comparable-rate evidence.** §17(e)(1) requires reasonable-and-fair compensation. The conduct lens reads whether the comparable-rate evidence is contemporaneous with the trades being evaluated, drawn from a reasonably comparable peer set, and carries the methodology disclosure the board can challenge.
- **Soft-dollar review against the fund-board's understanding.** The board approves soft-dollar use as part of the §15(c) deliberations. The conduct lens reads whether the surveillance findings on soft-dollar usage match what the board approved (use case, dollar magnitude, mixed-use allocation, provider list).
- **Sub-advised funds and the multiple-best-execution-duties problem.** Where the fund has a primary adviser and a sub-adviser, both owe best-execution duties; the surveillance must show the primary adviser's oversight of the sub-adviser's best-execution program is real, not just contractual.

#### Reg BI and Form CRS interaction

For BD-side surveillance touching retail recommendations:

- The artifact's conduct block records the surveillance findings on recommended-transaction execution quality separately from non-recommended. Reg BI's Care Obligation reads on the recommended population; the non-recommended population has best-execution duty under Rule 5310 but the customer-protection lens differs.
- The Form CRS relationship summary characterises the firm's routing arrangements; material divergence between the Form CRS characterisation and the actual routing pattern is a conduct finding for the surveillance to surface back to the CCO and to the team responsible for Form CRS accuracy.

#### What the conduct overlay does not cover

This overlay does not carry the Reg BI surveillance program itself, the Form CRS drafting and update process, or the broader UDAAP analysis that consumer-compliance carries on the consumer side (UDAAP applies to BDs only narrowly). Where a finding implicates Reg BI more centrally than best execution, the artifact flags it and routes to the appropriate Reg BI surveillance workflow.
