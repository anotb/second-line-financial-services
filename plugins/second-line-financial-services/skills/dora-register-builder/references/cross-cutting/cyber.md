# Cyber cross-cutting overlay — dora-register-builder

When the scope flags cyber, or whenever the register includes ICT services that touch network, security, identity, logging, or any control on a critical-or-important-function path, load this overlay alongside `references/source-anchors.md`. Cyber sits inside the DORA register by design; the schema has fields for it, and supervisors read those fields through an ICT-security lens.

## Why cyber is in scope for the register, not just for vendor diligence

The DORA Register of Information is not a generic third-party register. It is an ICT register, and ICT-security elements are first-class citizens of the schema, not a separate dataset:

- **ICT-service-type codes (ITS Annex taxonomy)** [verify Annex number] include security-specific service categories (managed detection and response, security operations, identity and access management, cryptography and key management, vulnerability management) alongside the broader infrastructure and software categories. These get coded explicitly; security services do not collapse into a generic "other" bucket.
- **Location of data at rest and location of data processing** (`b_05_ict_services` fields) are mandatory for many records. Cross-border data flows are a supervisory focus and the register is the firm's own statement of where its data physically sits per ICT service. Encryption-at-rest posture is implied by the location read; if the location is "intra-EU encrypted at rest with customer-managed keys" the register entry is one fact-pattern, "intra-US processing with vendor-managed keys" is a different fact-pattern, and the register surfaces the difference.
- **Sub-contractor-chain visibility** (`b_06_sub_contractors_chain`) is the structured surface for the supply-chain-cyber question. Where a critical-or-important-function ICT service is delivered through a chain, the register names the chain. The cyber question of "who actually touches our data and where" is read off this table.
- **Function-level recovery objectives** (`b_04_functions.rto`, `b_04_functions.rpo`) [verify ITS column] are cyber-adjacent: a cyber-incident-driven recovery is the predictable failure mode for which RTO and RPO matter most. The register-recorded RTO/RPO must be consistent with the firm's incident-response playbooks and the vendor's evidenced recovery test reports, not the vendor's marketing claim.
- **Contractual-arrangement fields** (`b_01_contractual_arrangement_general` and `b_02_contractual_arrangement_specific`) carry the existence of the security clauses required by **DORA Article 30** (rights of access, audit, sub-outsourcing chain visibility, exit-cooperation, security-incident-notification clock) [verify subarticle structure of 30(2) and 30(3)]. The register does not replicate the clause text; it records that the clause is present, and `contract-gap-review` carries the clause-text analysis.

## Operative cyber anchors that read into the register

- **DORA Article 9 — protection and prevention** [verify article number]. The ICT security policies, procedures, protocols, and tools that the firm must maintain. The register's ICT-services entries should reconcile against the firm's own ICT-asset inventory and the cyber-control coverage map; gaps surface as `data_quality_findings`.
- **DORA Articles 17 to 23 — ICT-related incident management, classification, and reporting** [verify article range]. Vendor incident-notification clocks (recorded operationally elsewhere) must be consistent with the firm's regulatory incident-reporting clock; the register's ICT-third-party identifier links the vendor to the incident-reporting pipeline.
- **DORA Articles 24 to 27 — digital operational resilience testing**, including the threat-led penetration testing (TLPT) framework for entities in scope [verify article range]. Where a register-listed ICT third party is in the scope of the firm's TLPT, that is an operational fact (not a register field) the firm's resilience-testing programme should map back to the register entries.
- **NIS2 Directive — Directive (EU) 2022/2555**. NIS2 operates alongside DORA: the **lex specialis** rule in NIS2 Article 4 [verify article number] means DORA prevails for financial entities on the matters DORA covers (ICT risk management, incident reporting, resilience testing, third-party risk, information sharing). NIS2 still applies for matters DORA does not cover and for non-financial group entities. For an EU financial-services group with non-financial subsidiaries (a payments-fintech group with a holding company that is a digital-services provider, an insurer with a tech-services subsidiary), the NIS2 register and the DORA register sit in parallel, with overlapping scope handled by the lex specialis rule. Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2022/2555/oj .
- **EBA Guidelines on ICT and security risk management (EBA/GL/2019/04)** [verify guideline number]. Pre-DORA cyber-and-ICT-risk frame for EU banks and payment service providers; superseded in many respects by DORA but still operative for the supervisory expectations on day-to-day ICT security controls that the register entries reflect.
- **ENISA threat-landscape and supply-chain-attack reports**. Public-source supervisory background; useful as the framing for sub-contractor-chain cyber findings recorded as `data_quality_findings.severity = high` against a critical-or-important-function arrangement.

## NIS2 alignment for EU entities

For an in-scope EU financial-services group:

- **Lex specialis posture**: DORA prevails on ICT risk management, ICT-related incident reporting, digital operational resilience testing, ICT third-party risk management, and information-sharing arrangements. NIS2 still bites for governance, security measures outside DORA's scope, and for non-financial group entities (a holding company classified as an essential or important entity under NIS2 Annexes I or II [verify Annex reference]).
- **Register interplay**: the DORA Register of Information is not a NIS2 artifact, but the underlying truth in the firm's TPRM data should support both. A financial-entity subsidiary files the DORA register; a non-financial sister entity files whatever NIS2 supply-chain-security reporting its host-state transposition requires; both reads sit on the same TPRM data backbone. The ICT-third-party identifier (LEI) is the join key.
- **Incident-reporting consistency**: vendor incident-notification clocks recorded against register entries should be set against the stricter of the DORA incident-reporting clock and the NIS2 transposition's reporting clock for the relevant group entity. The register field is operational reference, not a substitute for the legal obligation read; record the obligation in the firm's incident-response playbook and let the register carry the operational fact.

## What the cyber overlay expects in the register output

- **`b_03_ict_third_party_service_providers`**: cyber-relevant fields populated with evidence (country of head office and country of registration named explicitly, ultimate-parent identifier set, EU-presence flag set; type field uses the ITS taxonomy code that matches the actual service, not the vendor's marketing category). Hyperscaler entries flagged as CTPP candidates per Article 31 designation criteria [verify article number] even where designation has not yet occurred.
- **`b_05_ict_services`**: location of data at rest and location of data processing populated for every record; sensitivity-of-data indicator set against a documented data-classification scheme; ICT-service-type code drawn from the ITS taxonomy with the firm's internal-taxonomy mapping recorded outside the register.
- **`b_06_sub_contractors_chain`**: chain visibility to the depth required for critical-or-important-function services; `role_in_chain` distinguishes the actual service provider from the intermediary; locations populated.
- **`data_quality_findings`**: cyber-related gaps surfaced explicitly, with severity. Examples: missing data-processing-location for a critical-or-important-function arrangement; sub-contractor-chain truncated at first tier for a critical-or-important-function arrangement; ICT-service-type miscoded as a generic infrastructure code where the actual service is a security service that should be coded specifically.

## Common cyber-overlay pitfalls in register builds

- Treating the register as a procurement artifact and undercoding security services into a generic "other" or "software" ICT-service-type bucket.
- Leaving location-of-data-processing blank because the vendor uses a global-CDN architecture; the register expects the firm's read of where its data actually sits, with the cross-border flow flagged.
- Stopping sub-contractor-chain entries at the first tier for critical-or-important-function services because the vendor "does not disclose deeper". Missing entries are a `data_quality_findings.severity = high` item, not an absence.
- Inferring NIS2 obligations into the DORA register or vice versa. Two regulations, two artifacts; the lex specialis rule decides which set of obligations bites for which entity, and the register is the DORA filing.
- Setting register-recorded RTO and RPO from the vendor's marketing claim instead of the firm's evidenced impact-tolerance read for the supported important business service.
- Treating a clean cyber-control evidence pack from `vendor-diligence` as the register entry. The diligence pack is the input; the register is the structured filing. Each is a distinct artifact with its own audience.
