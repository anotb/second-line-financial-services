# Privacy cross-cutting overlay — dora-register-builder

When the scope flags privacy, or whenever the in-scope ICT third-party arrangements process personal data of investors, customers, employees, counterparties, or any other natural persons, load this overlay alongside `references/source-anchors.md`. For an EU-domiciled financial entity under DORA, that is essentially every register build: ICT services that touch core banking, asset servicing, transfer agency, fund accounting, payments, claims handling, customer-support, identity, monitoring, or analytics flow personal data. The narrow case where this overlay can be skipped is an ICT service that demonstrably processes zero personal data (pure compute-resource provisioning the firm does not extend to personal-data workloads, or a corporate-treasury cash-management feed with no natural-person counterparty content). Document the skip rationale; do not silently omit.

The overlay threads privacy through the register's existing fields. The DORA Register of Information remains an ICT-third-party register filed under Article 28; the GDPR Article 30 record of processing activities is the firm's separate privacy artifact owned by the DPO. The two artifacts share underlying truth about who processes what data where, and the register's data-residency, data-sensitivity, and sub-contractor-chain fields are where that shared truth surfaces in the supervisory filing.

## Operative anchors

- **GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679)**. The dominant privacy frame for EU register builds. Operative articles for register-relevant fields:
  - **Article 28** — processor obligations and the contractually-required content of the Article 28(3) processor agreement. The register's contractual-arrangement reference and sub-contractor chain are the structured surface; the clause-text analysis lives in `contract-gap-review`.
  - **Article 30** — records of processing activities. The DPO's Article 30 record is a separate artifact from the DORA register but the underlying data should reconcile; mismatches between the two on residency or sub-processor lists are findings.
  - **Article 32** — security of processing. The vendor security-evidence requirements that satisfy DORA Article 28 contractual-security obligations and GDPR Article 32 technical-and-organisational measures are largely the same evidence pack read through different lenses; the register entry assumes both reads succeed.
  - **Articles 44 to 49** — international data transfers. A `location_of_data_processing` outside the EEA needs a Chapter V transfer mechanism: SCCs (Commission Decision (EU) 2021/914), the EU-US Data Privacy Framework adequacy decision (Commission Decision of 10 July 2023) [verify current adequacy status; DPF is litigated], adequacy decisions for other third countries, binding corporate rules, or one of the Article 49 derogations (narrow). The register field records the location; the transfer mechanism is a contract-and-policy artifact the register points to via the source trace.
  - **Article 35** — data-protection-impact-assessment threshold for high-risk processing. Where the supported function hits the threshold, the DPIA reference belongs in the source trace.
  - **Article 25** — data protection by design and by default. The register's residency-and-classification fields are the operational surface for design choices made earlier.
  Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj .
- **Standard Contractual Clauses for transfers (Commission Decision (EU) 2021/914)**. The default Chapter V transfer mechanism for transfers from the EEA to a third country lacking adequacy. Module 2 (controller to processor) and Module 3 (processor to processor) are the modules most ICT third-party arrangements use. The register notes the residency; the SCCs sit at the contract level (read by `contract-gap-review`).
- **EU-US Data Privacy Framework (Commission Decision of 10 July 2023)** [verify current adequacy status]. DPF is the live adequacy bridge for transfers to certified US importers. Treat the status as a verifiable fact each cycle, not as fixed: the predecessor frameworks (Safe Harbor, Privacy Shield) were both invalidated by the CJEU (Schrems I and Schrems II); DPF is under litigation challenge. The register entry records location; the firm's transfer posture for US-located processing should be re-tested at each annual register refresh, and the source trace should cite the DPF adequacy decision plus any relevant CJEU update.
- **EDPB Recommendations on supplementary measures (Recommendations 01/2020)** [verify current version]. Post-Schrems II guidance on transfer-impact assessments and supplementary measures where SCCs alone do not bring third-country protection up to essential equivalence. The supplementary-measures analysis is not a one-time exercise; it is reviewed periodically and at each material change in the third-country surveillance regime. Where the register lists a third-country processing location for a critical-or-important-function ICT service, the firm's TIA reference belongs in the source trace.
- **EDPB Guidelines on processor agreements** (Guidelines 07/2020 on the concepts of controller and processor in the GDPR; Guidelines on Article 28) [verify guideline numbers]. Read by `contract-gap-review` for clause content; read here for the concept distinctions (controller / processor / joint controller) the register needs to flag correctly.
- **National supervisor data-protection guidance** that overlaps with outsourcing supervision. Used alongside the relevant sector overlay; the register reflects the residency, the supervisor-specific overlay carries the deeper read.
  - **CSSF Circular 22/806 on outsourcing arrangements** [verify circular reference] — Luxembourg supervisory expectations on cloud outsourcing and personal-data residency for credit institutions, investment firms, and fund managers; cross-references CNPD positions on personal-data flows.
  - **BaFin MaRisk AT 9 (outsourcing) and BAIT** [verify section] — German supervisory expectations on outsourcing including personal-data handling, with reference to the BfDI (federal DPA) and the Land DPAs.
  - **AMF and ACPR outsourcing guidance** [verify reference] — French supervisory expectations cross-referencing CNIL positions on personal-data flows and on cross-border processing.
  - **CBI Cross-Industry Guidance on Outsourcing** [verify reference] — Irish supervisory expectations cross-referencing DPC positions; relevant for many EU fund-administration and asset-servicing arrangements.
- **ISO/IEC 27018** [verify section] — public-cloud PII processor controls baseline. Used as a vendor-evidence anchor where the cloud sub-contractor's certification is in the diligence pack; the register notes the location, the certification sits in the source trace.
- **PCI DSS** where the ICT service touches payment-card data. Card-data residency and tokenisation posture are register-relevant where the supported function is card-acquiring, card-issuing, or card-processing.
- **Sector-specific personal-data anchors**:
  - **MiFID II (Directive 2014/65/EU) and MiFIR (Regulation (EU) 600/2014)** investor-data record-keeping obligations [verify article reference]. Investor PII held by an order-management or trade-reporting ICT third party is in scope and the residency field matters.
  - **AIFMD (Directive 2011/61/EU) and UCITS (Directive 2009/65/EC)** investor-data obligations carried through to fund administrators, transfer agents, and depositaries [verify article references]. Transfer-agency platforms hold KYC PII at scale; residency and sub-processor visibility are register-load-bearing.
  - **PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366)** [verify article reference] — payment-services personal-data handling, including strong-customer-authentication evidence held by ICT third parties.

## Evidence the privacy overlay expects in the register

The overlay does not add a new register table. It tightens the discipline on existing fields and adds entries to the source trace and the data-quality findings:

- **Data-classification field discipline.** Where personal data is in scope, `b_05_ict_services.sensitivity_of_data` reflects the classification under the firm's scheme, mapped to GDPR special-category status under Article 9 or criminal-conviction-data status under Article 10 where relevant. Leaving the field blank or at a generic "internal" where investor PII, employee PII, or customer PII clearly flows is a `data_quality_findings` entry at `severity = high` for critical-or-important-function arrangements, `medium` otherwise.
- **Data-residency field tied to a transfer-mechanism reference.** Where `location_of_data_processing` (or `location_of_data_at_rest`) sits outside the EEA, the source trace carries a pointer to the firm's transfer-mechanism artifact (SCCs, DPF reliance, BCRs, adequacy decision) and the relevant transfer-impact assessment. Sub-contractor chain entries with third-country processing carry the same pointer. A blank residency field for a service the vendor delivers from a global multi-region architecture is wrong, not minimal.
- **DPIA reference where Article 35 thresholds bite.** Where the supported function in `b_04_functions` triggers GDPR Article 35 (special-category data on a large scale, systematic monitoring, automated decision-making with legal effects, or another threshold the firm's DPIA scheme catches), the DPIA reference belongs in the source trace. The register itself does not carry the DPIA; it points to it.
- **Processor / sub-processor identification consistent across DORA and GDPR.** The DORA sub-outsourcing chain in `b_06_sub_contractors_chain` and the GDPR processor / sub-processor chain are the same chain read through different lenses. Inconsistencies between the register and the firm's GDPR Article 30 record (vendor on one but not the other; sub-processor list shorter on one side; residency value differs) are reconciliation findings, not silent overwrites.
- **Joint-controller scenarios flagged.** Where an arrangement is structurally a joint-controller relationship under GDPR Article 26 rather than controller-to-processor (some shared-services arrangements, some marketplace and payment-orchestrator arrangements, some intra-group governance constructs), the register entry should not silently treat the counterparty as a processor. Flag in `data_quality_findings` and route to `contract-gap-review` for the joint-controller-arrangement clause read.
- **Intra-group GDPR mechanism named.** Intra-group ICT arrangements remain reportable under DORA and remain subject to GDPR. The register's `intra_group_flag` should pair with a source-trace pointer to the operative GDPR mechanism: the intra-group Article 28 processor agreement, the intra-group controller-to-controller arrangement, or the BCRs covering the flow. Intra-group is not a GDPR exemption.
- **Vendor security-evidence dual read.** The vendor security-evidence pack that supports `b_03_ict_third_party_service_providers` (SOC 2, ISO 27001, ISO 27018, pen-test summaries, cloud-region attestations) is read for both DORA Article 28 contractual-security obligations and GDPR Article 32 technical-and-organisational-measures obligations. The register's relational entries assume both reads succeeded; gaps surface as `data_quality_findings` entries that route to `vendor-diligence` and `contract-gap-review`.

## Cross-link inside the plugin

- **`vendor-diligence` privacy overlay** carries the per-arrangement diligence depth on vendor privacy posture (DPA presence, sub-processor list, breach-notification chain, data-subject-rights handling). The register consumes the diligence record's outputs; the overlay here ensures the register entries match the diligence record's privacy facts.
- **`contract-gap-review` privacy overlay** carries the clause-text analysis on the Article 28 processor agreement, SCCs, BAA-equivalent provisions, and breach-notification timing. The register notes that the clauses exist (via the contractual-arrangement reference); the gap analysis lives there.
- **`exit-plan` privacy overlay** carries the data-return and data-destruction obligations on termination, including derivative artifacts (vector embeddings, fine-tune data, log archives) where AI-vendor processing is in scope. The register's B.04 critical-or-important-function entries link to exit plans; the privacy posture of the exit plan reads from there.
- **`resilience-testing-pack` privacy overlay** carries scenarios that surface personal-data exposure during failover or vendor-side incidents. Cross-border failover that routes data outside contractually permitted regions is a register-level fact (residency mismatch with operational reality) and a testing-design constraint.

## Common privacy-overlay pitfalls in register builds

- Treating GDPR as out-of-scope because "DORA covers it." DORA and GDPR are concurrent regimes with overlapping but distinct obligations; the register is a DORA artifact and the firm's Article 30 record is a separate GDPR artifact. Both apply.
- Treating the EU-US Data Privacy Framework adequacy as static. DPF is the live bridge; predecessors were invalidated by the CJEU, and DPF is under challenge. Re-test status at each annual refresh; do not freeze the assumption.
- Treating intra-group data flows as automatically covered. Intra-group remains in GDPR scope; an Article 28 processor agreement, controller-to-controller mechanism, or BCRs are still required. The DORA `intra_group_flag` is not a GDPR mechanism.
- Omitting `location_of_data_processing` for sub-contractor chain entries because the vendor uses a global multi-region architecture. The register expects the firm's read of where its data actually sits, with the cross-border flow flagged. "Global" is not a country code.
- Treating Schrems II SCC supplementary measures as one-time work. The TIA is reviewed periodically and at each material change in the third-country surveillance regime. The register's annual refresh is a natural review cadence.
- Assuming sector overlays (capital-markets investor data, payments-fintech card data, banking customer data) are covered without checking the firm's privacy-policy mapping. The privacy-policy mapping table sits in `references/firm-overlay.md`; consume it where present, do not assume.
- Conflating "personal data of natural persons employed by the counterparty" with "no personal data." Counterparty contact details, signatories, authorised users, and beneficial-owner records are personal data in B2B contexts. The register's `sensitivity_of_data` field for a counterparty-management ICT service should reflect that.
- Leaving `sensitivity_of_data` at "internal" or blank where investor PII, employee PII, or customer PII is in scope. This is the register-level expression of a classification failure upstream; surface as `data_quality_findings` rather than smooth over.
- Treating an Article 28 processor agreement as covering all flows. The Article 28 chain runs to sub-processors via flow-down; visibility into rank 2+ sub-processors is a separate contractual mechanic, and the register's `b_06_sub_contractors_chain` is where the visibility (or its absence) is recorded.
- Treating a vendor's GDPR-marketing posture as evidence. The contract is the operative document; trust-and-safety pages and certification badges modify the public default in both directions. The diligence record carries the contract-and-evidence read; the register entry assumes that read succeeded.
- Reading "we comply with GDPR" as compliance. The register surfaces the specific structured fields the regulation reads; generic compliance language does not satisfy the register's data-quality bar.
