## conduct

### Conduct cross-cutting overlay for fund-board-reporting

When the scope record flags `conduct` for the fund-board pack, this overlay sets the conduct-content for the named pack sections. Conduct in the fund-board context is the fiduciary lens — the adviser's fiduciary duty under §206 of the Investment Advisers Act, the affiliated-transaction conflict surface under §17 of the Investment Company Act, the §15(c) factor analysis itself (which is a fiduciary-bargain test), and the soft-dollar, brokerage, and revenue-sharing surfaces that drive fall-out benefits to the adviser and affiliates.

Loaded by: `fund-board-reporting/SKILL.md` when the scope flags conduct, when the pack covers a §15(c) renewal cycle (which always invokes the fiduciary lens), or when affiliated-transaction or soft-dollar items in the period rise to a level that warrants substantive board treatment beyond the standing affiliated-transactions table.

#### Source basis

- IA Act §206 — antifraud and fiduciary duty of the investment adviser; SEC Commission Interpretation Regarding Standard of Conduct for Investment Advisers (Release No. IA-5248, June 5, 2019).
- ICA §17 — affiliated transactions:
  - §17(a) — prohibitions on affiliated transactions.
  - §17(d) and Rule 17d-1 — joint transactions with affiliated persons.
  - §17(e) and Rule 17e-1 — affiliated brokerage; reasonable-and-fair commission determination; quarterly board procedure-adequacy review.
  - Rule 17a-7 — affiliated cross trades; pricing methodology; board procedure adoption and annual review.
  - Rule 17a-8 — affiliated mergers.
- ICA §15(c) — advisory contract approval and renewal under the Gartenberg / Jones v. Harris factor analysis; the §15(c) deliberation is itself the principal documented fiduciary-bargain record.
- Securities Exchange Act §28(e) — soft-dollar safe harbor; SEC Release No. 34-54165 (July 18, 2006) interpretive guidance on client commission practices.
- IA Act Rule 204A-1 — adviser code of ethics; reaches sub-advisers under fund-complex sub-adviser oversight loop.
- ICA Rule 17j-1 — fund code of ethics; access-person personal trading reporting; board approval.
- 12b-1 / Rule 12b-1 — fund distribution payments; board approval and ongoing oversight; inherently a fall-out-benefit consideration in §15(c).

#### What changes for conduct-flagged fund-board packs

##### 1. The §15(c) factor analysis as fiduciary-bargain record

The §15(c) renewal pack is itself the principal conduct artifact. The Gartenberg / Jones v. Harris factor analysis tests whether the advisory bargain is one that could have resulted from arm's-length negotiation. The conduct overlay reinforces:

- Profitability of the adviser from the fund relationship: methodology pin (direct expense allocation, indirect cost basis, capital usage). A bare profitability number without methodology is the §36(b) discovery finding pattern.
- Fall-out benefits to the adviser and affiliates: the catalogue must be substantive — soft-dollar credits and the eligibility analysis under §28(e); affiliated-broker revenue under Rule 17e-1; 12b-1 distribution flows to the adviser's distributor affiliate; affiliated-bank deposit balances and cash-management revenue; sub-adviser-relationship economics where the sub-adviser is affiliated; revenue-sharing flows from the adviser to financial intermediaries (where the adviser pays for distribution and shelf space).
- Comparative fees and expenses: peer-group selection methodology; address Jones v. Harris "comparable services" question for the adviser's institutional-account or sub-adviser-relationship fees against the fund-level fee for comparable services.
- Economies of scale and breakpoints: AUM growth analysis; written analysis of why no further breakpoint is appropriate where AUM has grown materially since the last breakpoint adjustment.
- Trustee questions and adviser responses: substantive challenge documented; recycled prior-year questions without period-specific updates is itself a finding pattern.

##### 2. Affiliated-transaction conflict surface

Affiliated transactions under §17 carry an inherent conflict that the board manages through procedure-adequacy determinations. The conduct overlay reinforces:

- Rule 17e-1 affiliated brokerage: aggregate commissions to affiliated brokers in the period; the board's quarterly procedure-adequacy determination (§17e-1(a)(3)) is on the cycle every cycle; commentary on any procedure exception or change.
- Rule 17a-7 affiliated cross trades: count and aggregate value; pricing methodology evidence for each cross trade against the §17a-7 conditions (current-market price determination; compliance with the four conditions); annual board review of the procedures (§17a-7(e)).
- §17(a) and Rule 17a-8 affiliated transactions more broadly: any affiliated merger or significant affiliated transaction in the period, with the board's §17 analysis.
- §17(d) and Rule 17d-1 joint transactions: any in-period joint transaction with an affiliated person and the SEC exemptive-order or Rule 17d-1 reliance basis.
- Related-person disclosures: any new related-person relationship in the period; any change in the §2(a)(19) interested-person status of a board member or officer.

##### 3. Soft-dollar and §28(e) disclosure

Soft-dollar use is a fiduciary-relevant board disclosure even where the adviser does not make affiliated-broker payments. The conduct overlay carries:

- Total commissions paid for research and brokerage during the period; allocation between research and execution.
- Adviser's eligibility analysis under §28(e) for the research and brokerage services received; the 2006 SEC interpretive release framework.
- MiFID II hard-dollar treatment for advisers with EU client coverage; any unbundling of the fund's commissions.
- Commission-sharing arrangements (CSA) and client-commission arrangements (CCA) in use.
- Any policy change in the period, including any change in eligible-research categories or eligible-brokerage categories.
- Soft-dollar credits as a fall-out benefit in the §15(c) analysis (cross-link to the §15(c) section).

##### 4. Code of ethics and personal-trading conduct

Rule 17j-1 (fund code of ethics) and Rule 204A-1 (adviser code of ethics) reach access persons of the fund and the adviser, including sub-advisers under the fund-complex sub-adviser oversight loop. The conduct overlay carries:

- Aggregate personal-trading violations and code of ethics violations in the period (count and severity; individual employee names do not appear in the pack).
- Material code of ethics violations that meet the §38a-1 material-compliance-matter threshold.
- Sub-adviser code of ethics violations reaching the fund-CCO oversight loop.
- Any code of ethics policy change in the period; CCO recommendation on adequacy.

##### 5. 12b-1 distribution arrangements

For funds with a Rule 12b-1 distribution plan, the conduct overlay carries:

- 12b-1 fee aggregate paid in the period and the use of the fee (distribution payments, shareholder services, broker-dealer compensation, intermediary payments).
- The board's quarterly review of payments under the 12b-1 plan and the annual continuance determination.
- The fall-out-benefit treatment of 12b-1 flows to the adviser's distributor affiliate (cross-link to §15(c) fall-out benefits).
- Any 12b-1 plan amendment in the period, including the board approval and the shareholder-vote requirement analysis.

##### 6. Marketing Rule conduct surface

Rule 206(4)-1 (Marketing Rule) is the adviser's conduct surface for performance presentation, hypothetical performance, testimonials and endorsements, third-party ratings, and case studies. Material findings affecting fund marketing land in the §38a-1 service-provider compliance program assessment for the adviser and in the §15(c) nature-extent-quality factor.

Detail of marketing-rule evidence and substantiation lives in `marketing-rule-evidence`.

##### 7. Fiduciary-relevant forward-looking content

The forward-looking commentary's conduct component covers items the trustees should be aware of:

- Pending or in-flight SEC adoption activity that bears on adviser conduct or fund-affiliate transactions.
- Recent enforcement themes affecting fund advisers (off-channel-communications enforcement, marketing-rule enforcement on hypothetical performance and testimonials, fee-litigation themes under §36(b)).
- SEC Division of Examinations focus areas for the coming cycle that bear on fiduciary or affiliated-transaction review.
- Any in-flight SEC examination or enforcement engagement with the adviser or the fund.

##### 8. Cross-link to other capability skills and to sector skills

- Adviser-firm conduct slice in the adviser ERM pack: `risk-committee-pack` with conduct cross-cutting overlay.
- Marketing-rule evidence and substantiation: `marketing-rule-evidence`.
- Best-execution governance for the brokerage-quality test under §206 fiduciary duty: `best-execution-surveillance`.
- Single-issue write-up for an affiliated-transaction exception or a code of ethics matter: `issue-writeup`.

#### Conduct-specific output content

Added to the fund-board pack content:

- §15(c) advisory contract renewal pack content (annual cycle): full Gartenberg / Jones v. Harris factor analysis as the central fiduciary-bargain record; methodology pin for profitability and peer-group; substantive trustee questions and adviser responses.
- Affiliated transactions section: Rule 17e-1 affiliated brokerage with quarterly procedure-adequacy determination; Rule 17a-7 cross trades with pricing methodology; §17(a) / Rule 17a-8 / §17(d) / Rule 17d-1 in-period activity; related-person disclosures.
- Soft-dollar / §28(e) disclosure: commission allocation; eligibility analysis; CSA/CCA; MiFID II treatment; policy change.
- Code of ethics summary: aggregate violations; material code matters; sub-adviser code matters; policy change.

- Forward-looking conduct items.

#### Common pitfalls in conduct-flagged fund-board packs

- §15(c) profitability number without a defined methodology. The methodology is the fiduciary-bargain record; the number alone fails the Gartenberg / Jones v. Harris substantive review.
- Fall-out benefits catalogued generically. The adviser's fall-out benefits include soft-dollar credits, affiliated-broker revenue, 12b-1 distribution flows, affiliated-bank deposit balances, cash-management revenue, sub-adviser-relationship economics, and revenue-sharing payments. A generic statement misses the substantive question.
- Trustee questions recycled from the prior year. Independent trustees' questions are the documented substantive challenge; period-specific updates demonstrate the §15(c) deliberation; recycled questions are themselves a finding pattern in §36(b) discovery.
- Rule 17a-7 cross trades reported as a count. Each trade should carry the pricing methodology evidence against the §17a-7 four conditions; the board's procedure adoption and annual review is required by §17a-7(e).
- Rule 17e-1 affiliated brokerage reported as an aggregate without the board's quarterly procedure-adequacy determination on file. The quarterly determination is a §17e-1 condition; the pack reflects whether the determination was made.
- Soft-dollar disclosure treated as a generic statement of policy. The commission allocation, eligibility analysis, and any policy change in the period is the substantive disclosure.
- Code of ethics violations reported with individual employee names. Aggregate counts and material-matter detail without individual identification is the standard; HR / Legal channels carry individual-employee detail.
- 12b-1 quarterly review and annual continuance determinations missing from the pack for funds with a 12b-1 plan. The plan's continuance is a board action; the pack carries the supporting record.
- Conflating §206 fiduciary duty (adviser-level) with §36(b) excessive-fee analysis (board-level deliberation under §15(c) factors). They run together but are not the same; the §15(c) record carries both lenses.

## cyber

### Cyber cross-cutting overlay for fund-board-reporting

When the scope record flags `cyber` for the fund-board pack, this overlay sets the cyber-risk content for the named pack sections. The cyber surface a fund board oversees runs through the Rule 38a-1 service-provider compliance program (the adviser, transfer agent, custodian, fund accountant, distributor, valuation designee, sub-advisers all sit on top of cyber control programs) and through the Reg S-P customer-information safeguards as amended in May 2024.

Loaded by: `fund-board-reporting/SKILL.md` when the scope flags cyber, when a material cyber event affecting the fund or fund shareholders has occurred in the period, or when a service-provider cyber event has reached the §38a-1 material-compliance-matter threshold. Cited and section-referenced anchors live in `references/source-anchors.md`.

#### Source basis

- SEC Reg S-P amendments (May 16, 2024): customer-information safeguarding; written incident-response program; 30-day customer notice posture; service-provider oversight in incident response. Compliance dates phased by entity size. [verify current compliance dates]
- SEC Cybersecurity Risk Management Rule for Advisers and Funds (proposed February 9, 2022) — current state must be verified at pack-write time. [verify current state — re-proposed, withdrawn, or pending]
- SEC cyber disclosure rules for public-company issuers (Reg S-K Item 106; Form 8-K Item 1.05) — apply to the fund's adviser parent where the parent is a public-company issuer. Final rule July 26, 2023; compliance dates phased.
- NYDFS 23 NYCRR Part 500 (Second Amendment, effective November 1, 2023) — applies to NYDFS-licensed entities including BDs and IAs licensed in New York; 72-hour cybersecurity event notification under §500.17.
- NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 (February 2024) and FFIEC IT Examination Handbook for substantive control framing.
- ICA Rule 38a-1 — fund and service-provider compliance program; material compliance matters definition (38a-1(e)(2)) reaches cyber events that constitute violations of the federal securities laws or weaknesses in the policies and procedures.

#### What changes for cyber-flagged fund-board packs

##### 1. Fund-shareholder customer-information events under Reg S-P

Reg S-P (May 2024) covers customer-information events affecting fund shareholders. The pack carries:

- Any customer-information event in the period: nature of the event, affected systems, types of customer information affected (account number, transaction history, taxpayer identification number, beneficiary information), customer count affected, the customer-notice posture and timing (no later than 30 days after becoming aware that unauthorized access has occurred or is reasonably likely to have occurred).
- The fund's incident-response program reference and any program change in the period.
- Service-provider-driven events: the transfer agent, custodian, distributor, or sub-transfer-agent surface holds the bulk of fund-shareholder customer information; an event at the service provider triggers the fund's notice obligation when fund-shareholder customer information was affected. The pack carries the service-provider event detail, the contractual notification posture, and the fund's notice obligation analysis.

Reg S-P customer-notice timing is binding (30 days after becoming aware); the pack reflects whether the fund met the timing and any escalation that occurred.

##### 2. Service-provider cyber events feeding the §38a-1 material compliance matter assessment

A cyber event at a service provider may meet the §38a-1 material-compliance-matter threshold if it constitutes a violation of the federal securities laws, a violation of the service provider's compliance policies and procedures, or a weakness in the design or implementation of the policies and procedures. The pack carries:

- Service-provider cyber events in the period meeting the firm's reporting threshold.
- The fund CCO's assessment of whether the event is a §38a-1 material compliance matter for the service provider (and accordingly for the fund).
- The service provider's remediation status and the parent CCO's oversight evidence (testing, third-party reports, attestations supplemented by independent verification).
- Service-provider contract review for incident-notification adequacy: whether the contract requires the service provider to notify the fund or adviser within a defined timeframe, what escalation rights the fund has, and what audit rights the fund retains.

##### 3. Public-parent SEC cyber disclosure considerations (Item 1.05, Item 106)

Where the fund's adviser is a subsidiary of a public-company issuer, SEC cyber disclosure rules (Reg S-K Item 106 for annual disclosure, Form 8-K Item 1.05 for material incident disclosure) apply at the public-parent level. The fund-board pack carries:

- Brief reference to any 8-K Item 1.05 disclosure made by the public parent in the period that touches the adviser or the fund.
- Brief reference to the public parent's most recent Item 106 disclosure where it bears on the adviser's program (the disclosure is the public parent's, not the fund's, but the board's oversight context benefits from the reference).
- The disclosure-process referral status for any in-period event that may rise to the Item 1.05 disclosure threshold for the public parent.

The fund board does not make the public-parent disclosure decision; the parent's disclosure committee or equivalent process owns that. The pack flags the event and the disclosure-process referral.

##### 4. Cyber as a Rule 38a-1 service-provider program element

The CCO's annual written report under Rule 38a-1(a)(4)(iii) addresses the operation of the fund's compliance program and each service provider's compliance program. Cyber is a program element for every service provider in scope. The pack carries:

- Each service provider's cybersecurity program adequacy as part of the per-service-provider 38a-1 compliance program assessment.
- Material cyber-control changes at each service provider in the period.
- Independent attestation status (SOC 2 Type II reports for the major service-provider categories; third-party penetration test results where shared; incident-response tabletop participation).
- Any cyber-related supervisory finding affecting a service provider (SEC EXAMS finding, NYDFS Part 500 examination finding, FFIEC finding for the bank-affiliated custodian).

##### 5. Sub-adviser cyber oversight

Sub-advisers carry their own cyber surface. The pack treats sub-adviser cyber as a sub-adviser-compliance-program element under the parent CCO's 38a-1 oversight:

- Sub-adviser cyber program adequacy assessment evidence.
- Sub-adviser cyber events in the period and any material events.
- Sub-adviser cyber-attestation status (SOC 2, independent assessment, parent-CCO testing where conducted).
- Sub-adviser code of ethics and personal-trading platform cyber posture, given the cyber surface around personal-trading data.

##### 6. Cyber-related forward-looking content

The forward-looking commentary's cyber component covers items the trustees should be aware of:

- Reg S-P May 2024 amendments compliance phase-in for the fund and the service-provider population; the fund's program-update status and the service-provider program-update verification status.
- SEC Cybersecurity Risk Management Rule for Advisers and Funds — current state of the proposal and the fund's contingency planning if the rule is finalized.
- NYDFS Part 500 Second Amendment phased compliance dates affecting NY-licensed BDs and IAs in the fund's service-provider population.
- Threat-environment forward view to the extent relevant to the fund or its service providers (sector-specific intelligence, regulator-issued advisories).

- Public-company cyber disclosure decision and 8-K Item 1.05 trigger analysis for the public parent: `cyber-disclosure-readiness`.
- Vendor cyber concentration view (the fund's service-provider cyber surface is a concentration question): `concentration-risk-review` with capital-markets sector overlay.
- Single-issue write-up for a material cyber event meeting §38a-1 threshold: `issue-writeup`.

#### Cyber-specific output content

Added to the fund-board pack content:

- Cyber and operational resilience events section (deck slide and Word annex section): events affecting the fund or fund shareholders in the period; service-provider cyber events meeting the firm reporting threshold; Reg S-P customer-notice posture for any in-period customer-information event; public-parent disclosure-referral status where applicable.
- Service-provider compliance summary: each in-scope service provider's cyber-program adequacy assessment; SOC 2 / independent attestation status; material cyber-program change in the period.
- Forward-looking cyber items: regulatory horizon (Reg S-P phase-in, SEC cyber rule status, NYDFS phased dates) and threat-environment forward view where relevant.

#### Common pitfalls in cyber-flagged fund-board packs

- Treating Reg S-P customer-notice timing as a service-provider problem. The fund retains the customer-notice obligation when fund-shareholder customer information was affected, regardless of which party in the chain held the information at the moment of the event.
- Sub-adviser and service-provider cyber attestation accepted without independent verification. The 38a-1 oversight expectation is that the parent CCO reasonably believes the program is adequate; attestation alone does not satisfy this.
- Public-parent 8-K Item 1.05 disclosure decisions reported in the fund-board pack as if they bind the disclosure committee. The disclosure-committee process is separate; the pack flags the event and the disclosure-process referral.
- Cyber rated "stable" in the operational-resilience section while material cyber events occurred in the period, without commentary that explains the apparent inconsistency.
- Service-provider cyber events reported only as a count. The board needs the conflict shape: which service provider, which information surface, the contractual notification posture, the fund's notice obligation analysis, and the remediation status.
- Forward-looking cyber commentary that is generic ("emerging cyber threats remain elevated"). Replace with named items the board can act on: Reg S-P implementation status, SEC cyber rule status with the firm's contingency posture, named threat-environment items where they bear on the fund's service-provider population.
